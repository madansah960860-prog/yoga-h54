About Us & Meet the Teacher

This site is a free library of plain-language pose guides and short sequences for people practicing at home, written so that a chair, a wall or a block is never a footnote. Here is why it exists, how it is made, how it pays for itself, and who is behind it.

Meet the teacher How we are funded

Why this site exists

Search for almost any yoga pose online and you will usually find a photo of someone who already does it easily, a sentence or two of instructions, and a list of benefits nobody can check. If your hamstrings are tight, your knees are older than they used to be, or getting down to the floor takes some planning, that page has very little to say to you.

This site is built the other way around. Each guide explains a pose the way a patient teacher would in a small class: what it asks of your body, how to come into it step by step, the mistakes almost everyone makes, and three or four complete versions using a chair, a wall, a block or a strap. The guided sequences put those poses in order with a timer, so you can practice without guessing what comes next.

Everything instructional is free, and it stays free. We would rather you practiced than paid.

Our approach

Five principles shape every page. If you ever find one we have broken, please tell us.

  1. Every version is the pose. A chair, a wall, a block or a strap changes where the floor is. It does not make a pose smaller. Supported versions get the same space, the same care and the same heading as the floor version.
  2. Mechanics, not miracles. We describe what moves, what lengthens, where your weight goes and what you might notice. We do not promise that a pose will do anything for a health condition, and we never frame practice around weight, shape or appearance.
  3. Arches over rectangles. A design note: every photograph here sits in an arch, a nod to the adobe doorways and long afternoon shadows of the desert where this site began.
  4. Honest money. Ads, affiliate links and sponsorships are labeled where they appear, not tucked into the footer. Exactly how the site is funded is set out further down this page.
  5. Calm pacing. Nothing here counts down, tracks streaks or hurries you. Sequences have a pause button, pages leave room to breathe, and motion is slow, or switched off entirely if your device asks for less motion.
Every version is the pose.

Meet the teacher

Founder and lead teacher

Rosa Delgado

Rosa Delgado started this site after years of teaching small beginner classes in community rooms, where students arrived in work clothes, straight from a shift or a school pickup, and where a stack of folding chairs was the most useful prop in the building.

She came to yoga as a stiff, skeptical beginner herself, and she has spent her teaching life on the students who feel least welcome in a typical class: people starting later in life, people who cannot easily get down to the floor, people who have been told they are "not flexible enough." Her classes lean on chairs and walls as the main event rather than a fallback, and her favorite moment in any class is when someone realizes that the version at the wall is the same pose.

On this site she writes or reviews every pose guide and sequence, tests each chair, wall and prop version in her own practice before it is published, and reads the questions readers send about cues and setup.

Training: She has completed a 200-hour and a 300-hour yoga teacher training.

!

A yoga teacher, not a clinician

She is not a doctor, physical therapist or clinician, and this site does not give medical advice. Our guides describe how poses move the body. For questions about your own health, an injury or a condition, please talk with a qualified healthcare professional, and read our Health & Safety Disclaimer.

Editorial standards

A guide you follow with your own body has to be right. This is how we try to make sure it is, and what we do when it is not.

How guides are researched

Each guide starts from teaching practice: the cues, common mistakes and supported versions that work for beginners in real classes. Anatomy is checked against standard anatomy references and written in plain words: which muscles work, which lengthen, which joints move and where your weight goes.

How guides are reviewed

Before publication, every pose guide and sequence is practiced start to finish in each of its versions, including the chair and wall options, and read for clarity by someone who did not write it. Published guides are reviewed again at least once a year.

Safety first

Every pose page has a block naming who should take care with the pose or avoid it, a reminder to work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance, and a link to the Health & Safety Disclaimer. When two cues would both work, we print the more conservative one.

Corrections

Found an error? Tell us through the contact page with the topic "Correction or feedback." We fix factual errors promptly, and when a correction changes instructions or safety guidance, we say so on the page and update its date.
