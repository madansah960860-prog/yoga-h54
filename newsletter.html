What arrives in your inbox

Each issue has the same three parts in the same order, so you can skim to the one you want. It is written by the same teacher who writes the pose guides, in the same plain language.

  1. One pose, explained. A single pose taken apart the way our guides do it: how to come into it, which muscles work and which lengthen, the cue that tends to make it click, and a chair or wall version that is every bit the pose.
  2. One 10-minute sequence. A short practice for that morning or evening, with hold times written out and a supported swap for every pose. Print it, or follow it on the site with the timer.
  3. One practical idea. Something small and useful for home practice: where to put a chair so it cannot slide, what to use if you do not own a strap, or what a cue you keep hearing in class is actually asking you to do.

There are no promises about what yoga will do for your health or your body, only clear descriptions of what moves, what stretches and what you might notice.

A sample issue, outlined

This is an example of how a typical issue is laid out. The poses and topics change every time.

  1. A short hello. Two or three sentences on what this issue covers and why.
  2. Pose of the issue: Bridge Pose Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana. Where to set your feet, what the backs of the legs and the hips are doing as you lift, and a supported version with a block under the sacrum. Links to the full Bridge Pose guide.
  3. The 10-minute sequence: a floor-based evening practice. Child's Pose Bālāsana, Cat-Cow Mārjaryāsana-Bitilāsana, Bridge, a reclined twist Supta Matsyendrāsana, legs up the wall Viparīta Karaṇī and a few minutes of rest, each with a hold time and a chair swap.
  4. One practical idea. How to stack two firm pillows so they hold steady under your sacrum in Bridge, if you do not own a block yet.
  5. From the guides. One link to a longer read, such as How to Read a Yoga Cue.
  6. Sponsor, only if there is one. When an issue has a sponsor, that section is labeled "Sponsored" in the email and kept separate from the instruction. Sponsors never write or edit the pose, the sequence or any safety note.
  7. The footer. Who sent the email, our mailing address, and a one-click unsubscribe link.

What we will never send

A practice letter should be something you are glad to open. These are the lines we hold, issue after issue.

  • No health or body promises. We describe what a pose asks of your muscles and joints, not what it will do for a condition, your weight or your shape.
  • No pressure. No streak counters, no countdown offers, no "last chance" subject lines.
  • No extra mail. One issue every other Sunday, plus a single confirmation email when you sign up. That is all.
  • No selling your address. We never sell or rent your email address to anyone.
  • No hidden sponsorships. Newsletter sponsorships, when present, are labeled "Sponsored" in the email.
