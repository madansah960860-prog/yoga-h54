Desk Reset

Fourteen short poses you can do in a sturdy chair or standing right beside it. You breathe, move your spine forward, back, sideways and around, stretch your hips and calves, and finish with a short rest in your seat. No mat, no floor and no change of clothes.

Start the timer
Total duration
8 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Poses
14
Props required
Sturdy chair without wheels. Desk or wall optional
i

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. Move within a range that feels comfortable, stop anything that hurts, and read the Health & Safety Disclaimer before you begin.

Pose 1 of 14

Seated breath

0:40

Sit toward the front of the chair with both feet flat and your hands on your thighs. Breathe slowly through your nose and let each exhale run a little longer than the inhale.

Sequence complete. Take a breath or two before you get back to work.

Pause any time. If a pose does not feel right today, skip it or use the swap listed below.

Pose-by-pose order

Hold times add up to exactly eight minutes: nine poses in the chair, three standing beside your desk, then two more back in your seat. Take your time between them.

i

Set up your chair first

  • Use a sturdy chair without wheels or a swivel base. Push its back against a wall, or stand its legs on a rug, so it cannot slide.
  • Sit toward the front edge with both feet flat. If your feet do not reach the floor, rest them on a thick book or a folded blanket.
  • For the standing poses, use a desk that does not roll or tip. If yours does, use a clear stretch of wall instead.

  1. Seated breath

    • Sit toward the front of the seat, feet flat and hip-width apart, hands resting on your thighs.
    • Breathe in and out through your nose, letting each exhale run slightly longer than the inhale.
    0:40

  2. Neck and shoulder rolls

    • Roll your shoulders up, back and down five or six times, slowly.
    • Let your right ear drift toward your right shoulder, then your left ear toward your left. Never force or bounce.
    • Leave out full head circles and keep your head from dropping back.
    0:40

  3. Seated Cat-Cow Mārjaryāsana-Bitilāsana

    • Hands on your knees. Inhale, lift your chest and let your back arch gently.
    • Exhale, round your spine and let your chin drift toward your chest.
    0:40

  4. Seated side bend, right arm up

    • Left hand on the seat beside your hip. Inhale the right arm up; exhale and lean to the left.
    • Keep both sitting bones heavy on the chair so the bend comes from your waist.
    0:30

  5. Seated side bend, left arm up

    • Switch sides: right hand down, left arm up and over to the right.
    0:30

  6. Seated twist to the right Parivṛtta Sukhāsana

    • Sit tall on an inhale. On an exhale, turn your chest to the right, left hand on your right knee, right hand on the seat or the side of the backrest.
    • Knees and hips keep facing forward. Turn from your ribs and use your hands only lightly.
    0:30

  7. Seated twist to the left Parivṛtta Sukhāsana

    • Unwind through center, then turn to the left with the same easy effort.
    0:30

  8. Seated figure-four, right ankle crossed

    • Cross your right ankle over your left thigh, just above the knee, and flex the right foot.
    • Sit tall, or hinge forward a little with a long back. You may notice a stretch in the outer right hip.
    0:30

  9. Seated figure-four, left ankle crossed

    • Switch legs. If the knee complains, rest the ankle lower, on your shin.
    0:30

  10. Half Dog at the desk or wall Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

    • Stand facing a desk that will not move, or a wall. Place your palms on the desk edge or on the wall at about hip height.
    • Walk your feet back and hinge at your hips until your arms and back make one long line. Bend your knees as much as you like.
    0:40

  11. Standing calf stretch, right leg back

    • Hands on the desk or wall. Step your right foot back about two feet and press the right heel down.
    • Bend the front knee and keep the back leg straight. You may notice the stretch in your right calf.
    0:30

  12. Standing calf stretch, left leg back

    • Switch legs. Toes of both feet point straight ahead.
    0:30

  13. Chair-supported forward fold

    • Sit down with your feet a little wider than your hips. Hinge forward from your hips and rest your forearms on your thighs.
    • If it is comfortable, let your arms and head hang toward the floor. Come up slowly, head last.
    • If you are prone to dizziness, keep your forearms on your thighs and your head above your heart.
    0:40

  14. Seated rest

    • Sit back in the chair with your hands in your lap and your feet flat.
    • Notice your breath for a few rounds before you return to what you were doing.
    0:40

Beginner modification path

Every pose in Desk Reset already happens in or beside a chair. If a shape still does not suit you today, swap in a smaller or fully seated version. Each swap keeps the same movement and only changes how far you go or where your weight sits. Use the same sturdy chair without wheels, pushed against a wall, for every swap.

Smaller and fully seated swaps for each pose in the Desk Reset sequence
In the sequenceSmaller or fully seated swap
Neck and shoulder rollsShoulder rolls only, with your head still. Or turn your head slowly to look over one shoulder, then the other.
Seated Cat-CowKeep the movement in your upper back: widen and lift your chest on the inhale, soften it on the exhale.
Seated side bend (both sides)Keep both hands on the seat and simply lean, or rest the lifted hand on top of your head.
Seated twist (both sides)Turn only as far as your hands can stay soft. Let your hands rest on your thighs instead of the chair.
Seated figure-four (both sides)Rest your ankle on your shin instead of your thigh, or keep both feet on the floor with your knees wide.
Half Dog at the desk or wallPlace your hands higher on the wall so the angle is shallower, or stay seated and slide your forearms forward along the desk.
Standing calf stretch (both sides)Stay seated, extend one leg with the heel on the floor, and gently draw your toes toward you.
Chair-supported forward foldFold only as far as your forearms on your thighs, or rest your forearms and forehead on the desk.

The first week

  • Practice the seated poses first. Poses 1 to 9 take exactly five minutes and never leave the chair.
  • Use any swap from the table, and pause the timer whenever you need to. There is nothing to catch up on.

When it feels familiar

  • Add the three standing poses at the desk or wall, then sit back down for the fold and rest.
  • If one side felt different, press Previous and repeat it. Keep any swap you prefer; both paths are the full sequence.

Practice with care

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. Check with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition. If you have a neck or shoulder injury, keep the neck movements very small or leave them out, and if you have had a hip replacement, ask your surgeon before crossing one ankle over the other knee. Stop any pose that causes pain, numbness or tingling, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. Read the Health & Safety Disclaimer.
