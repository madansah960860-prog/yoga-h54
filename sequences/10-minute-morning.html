10-Minute Morning

Twelve beginner poses that move your spine forward, back and side to side, wake up the legs, and finish with two balance poses and a short rest. Use the timer below or print the list.

Start the timer
Total duration
10 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Poses
12
Props required
None. Optional chair and folded blanket
i

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. Move within a range that feels comfortable, stop anything that hurts, and read the Health & Safety Disclaimer before you begin.

Pose 1 of 12

Easy Seat

Sukhāsana

1:00

Sit tall on a folded blanket or a chair. Breathe slowly through your nose and let each exhale run a little longer than the inhale.

Sequence complete. Take a moment before you stand up.

Pause any time. If a pose does not feel right today, skip it or use the swap listed below.

Pose-by-pose order

Hold times add up to exactly ten minutes. The order travels from sitting, to hands and knees, to kneeling, up to standing and back down to the floor, so you change level only a handful of times. Move between poses slowly; there is no prize for speed.

i

Set up before you start

  • Clear a space about 6 feet long and 2 feet wide, close enough to a bare wall that you can touch it during the balance poses.
  • Fold a blanket or a large towel. You will sit on it in Easy Seat and kneel on it in the lunges.
  • If the floor is not an option today, push a sturdy chair without wheels against the wall and follow the chair path in the table below.

  1. Easy Seat Sukhāsana

    • Sit on a folded blanket so your knees are at or below your hips, or sit on a chair with feet flat.
    • Breathe through your nose, letting the exhale lengthen slightly.
    • If your knees float high or your lower back rounds, sit higher: on two folded blankets, a cushion or the chair.
    1:00

  2. Cat-Cow Mārjaryāsana-Bitilāsana

    • Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips.
    • Inhale to lift chest and tailbone; exhale to round from the tailbone up.
    • Let your head follow the spine: gaze forward on the inhale and toward your knees on the exhale. Slide the blanket under your knees if the floor is hard.
    1:00

  3. Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

    • Knees bent, sitting bones high, spine long.
    • Pedal the feet for the first few breaths if your calves feel stiff.
    • Lower your knees to Tabletop whenever you need a breath. When you are ready, step your right foot between your hands for the lunge.
    0:45

  4. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana

    • Step the right foot between your hands and lower the left knee onto padding.
    • Front knee stacks over the ankle; hips ease forward.
    • If your back knee is sensitive, fold the blanket twice so it is thicker.
    0:45

  5. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana

    • Return to Down Dog or Tabletop, then step the left foot forward.
    • Hands on the front thigh or on blocks either side of the foot.
    • Notice whether this side feels different, without trying to make the two sides match.
    0:45

  6. Mountain Pose with arm sweeps Tāḍāsana

    • Walk to the front of the mat and roll up to standing.
    • Inhale arms up overhead; exhale arms down by your sides.
    • Press both feet evenly into the floor and keep your shoulders soft. If lifting your arms overhead pinches, sweep them only to shoulder height.
    1:00

  7. Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana

    • Hinge at the hips with soft knees.
    • Let the head hang heavy; hands rest on shins, blocks or a chair seat.
    • To move into Chair Pose, bend your knees deeply and lift your chest with a long back.
    0:45

  8. Chair Pose Utkaṭāsana

    • Feet hip-width apart; sit the hips back and down.
    • Knees track over the second toes; weight stays in the heels.
    • Arms forward at shoulder height ask less of your shoulders than arms overhead. Stand up and rest whenever your legs ask for it.
    0:30

  9. Tree Pose, right foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana

    • Right sole rests on the left ankle, calf or inner thigh, never on the knee.
    • Gaze at one still point; a hand on the wall is fine.
    • Keep your hands at your chest or on your hips. Reach them overhead only if the balance already feels steady.
    0:45

  10. Tree Pose, left foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana

    • Switch sides. Notice which side feels steadier without judging it.
    • Left sole on the right ankle, calf or inner thigh, never on the knee.
    • Lower the foot slowly and stand on both feet for a breath before you kneel down.
    0:45

  11. Child's Pose Bālāsana

    • Knees wide or together, hips toward heels.
    • Forehead on the mat, a block or stacked fists.
    • Use a hand on the wall or chair to kneel down from standing. If your hips do not reach your heels, slide the folded blanket between them.
    1:00

  12. Seated rest Sukhāsana

    • Return to a comfortable seat with your eyes open or closed.
    • Notice your breath for a few rounds and how your body feels now compared with ten minutes ago.
    • Come up to standing slowly, through hands and knees if that is easier.
    1:00

Beginner modification path

If getting down to the floor, balancing or bearing weight on your hands is not comfortable yet, swap in these versions. They are complete poses, not consolation prizes. Many people practice the chair path for weeks before moving any part of it to the floor, and some keep it for good.

Supported swaps for each pose in the 10-Minute Morning sequence
In the sequenceChair or wall swap
Easy SeatSit on the front half of a chair, feet flat and hip-width apart.
Cat-CowSeated Cat-Cow: hands on your knees, arch and round the spine while seated.
Downward-Facing DogHalf Dog at the wall: palms on the wall at hip height, walk back into an L shape.
Low Lunge (both sides)Standing lunge holding the back of a chair, or seated sideways on a chair with one leg extended back.
Mountain Pose arm sweepsThe same arm sweeps from a seated position.
Standing Forward FoldSeated fold: sit tall, then hinge forward over your thighs, forearms resting on your legs.
Chair PoseHover just above the seat of a chair, or hold the chair back for support.
Tree Pose (both sides)Stand side-on to a wall with one hand on it, or keep the lifted foot's toes on the floor.
Child's PoseSeated rest: fold forward over a pillow on your lap, or rest your forearms on a table.

Week one

  • Practice the whole sequence with the chair and wall swaps.
  • Pause the timer whenever you need to; there is no catching up to do.
  • Read the pose list once before your first session, so you know what comes next and can set the chair and blanket where you need them.

When it feels steady

  • Move one or two poses to the floor version, and keep the rest supported.
  • Keep any swap that feels better for your body. Both paths are the full practice.
  • When ten minutes starts to feel short, try Gentle Full Practice on a morning with more time. It opens with the same first poses.

Practice with care

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. Check with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition. If your wrists are sensitive, use the wall version of Downward-Facing Dog and practice Cat-Cow seated. If your knees are sensitive, pad them generously or use the chair versions of the lunges and Child's Pose. If you are prone to dizziness, keep a hand on the wall for Tree Pose and come up from forward folds slowly. Stop any pose that causes pain, numbness or tingling, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. Read the Health & Safety Disclaimer.
