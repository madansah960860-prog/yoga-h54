Gentle Full Practice

A complete beginner class in forty minutes. You arrive with a few minutes of breathing, warm up on hands and knees, work through a standing series with Warrior II, Triangle and Tree, take a gentle backbend, settle into seated and reclined stretches, and finish with seven minutes of rest.

Start the timer
Total duration
40 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Poses
25
Props required
Mat, 2 blocks, strap, blanket. Chair and wall optional
i

This class is general movement education, not medical advice. It includes standing balance, a backbend and time on your back, so read the cautions at the bottom of this page and the Health & Safety Disclaimer before you begin.

Pose 1 of 25

Easy Seat arrival

Sukhāsana

4:00

Sit on a folded blanket so your knees are at or below your hips. Breathe through your nose. For the last minute, count four on the inhale and four on the exhale.

Class complete. Roll onto your side and take a moment before you sit up.

Pause any time. If a pose does not feel right today, skip it or use the swap listed below.

Pose-by-pose order

Hold times add up to exactly forty minutes, arranged in six parts. Each part builds on the one before, so if you need to shorten the class, cut from the middle and keep the arrival and the final rest.

Set up before you start

  • Clear enough room to step your feet three to four feet apart along your mat for Warrior II and Triangle.
  • Put two blocks, a strap and a folded blanket beside the mat so you never have to leave it.
  • If you plan to use chair swaps, choose a sturdy chair without wheels and push it against a clear wall.

  1. Easy Seat arrival Sukhāsana

    • Sit on a folded blanket so your knees are at or below your hips, or on a chair with your feet flat.
    • Breathe through your nose. For the last minute, count four in and four out.
    4:00

  2. Cat-Cow Mārjaryāsana-Bitilāsana

    • Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips, a blanket under the knees if you like.
    • Inhale to lift chest and tailbone; exhale to round from the tailbone up. Start small and let the range grow.
    2:00

  3. Child's Pose Bālāsana

    • Knees wide or together, hips toward heels, forehead on the mat or a block.
    1:00

  4. Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

    • Tuck your toes and lift your hips up and back.
    • Bend your knees enough that your spine stays long. Pedal the feet if your calves feel stiff.
    1:00

  5. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana

    • Step the right foot between your hands and lower the left knee onto a folded blanket.
    • Front knee stacks over the ankle; hands on blocks; hips ease forward.
    1:00

  6. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana

    • Return to Down Dog or hands and knees, then step the left foot forward.
    1:00

  7. Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana

    • Walk your hands back to your feet, feet hip-width apart.
    • Soft knees, head heavy, hands on your shins or on blocks.
    1:00

  8. Mountain Pose with arm sweeps Tāḍāsana

    • Roll up slowly to standing, head last.
    • Inhale your arms up, exhale them down. Stand still for the final few breaths and feel your weight spread across both feet.
    1:30

  9. Warrior II, right knee bent Vīrabhadrāsana II

    • Step your feet about three and a half feet apart. Turn your right toes to the front of the mat and your left toes in slightly.
    • Bend the right knee toward a position over the ankle. Arms reach long at shoulder height; gaze past the right fingertips.
    1:00

  10. Warrior II, left knee bent Vīrabhadrāsana II

    • Turn to face the other way and repeat. Your front knee tracks over your second toe.
    1:00

  11. Extended Triangle, right side Utthita Trikoṇāsana

    • From Warrior II on the right, straighten the right leg without locking the knee.
    • Reach forward, then tip down: right hand on the shin or a block, left arm reaching up. Both sides of your waist stay long.
    1:00

  12. Extended Triangle, left side Utthita Trikoṇāsana

    • Turn to the left side and repeat.
    • Look down at the floor if turning your head up strains your neck.
    1:00

  13. Tree Pose, right foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana

    • Step to the top of the mat. Balance on the left foot and rest the right sole on the ankle, calf or inner thigh, never on the knee.
    • A fingertip on the wall is welcome.
    1:00

  14. Tree Pose, left foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana

    • Switch sides. Notice which side feels steadier without judging it.
    1:00

  15. Cobra Pose Bhujaṅgāsana

    • Lower to your belly, hands under your shoulders, tops of the feet pressing down.
    • Inhale to lift your chest a few inches; exhale to lower. Repeat three or four times, or stay low and hold.
    • Keep your elbows bent and close to your ribs. The lift comes from your back, not from pushing hard with your hands.
    1:00

  16. Child's Pose Bālāsana

    • Press back toward your heels and let your low back round gently.
    1:00

  17. Seated Forward Bend Paścimottānāsana

    • Sit on a folded blanket with your legs forward. Loop a strap around the balls of your feet.
    • Hinge forward from your hips with a long spine and bent knees. You may notice a stretch along the backs of your legs.
    2:30

  18. Easy Seated Twist, to the right Parivṛtta Sukhāsana

    • Sit cross-legged. Inhale to sit tall; exhale to turn right, left hand on the right knee, right fingertips on the floor behind you.
    1:00

  19. Easy Seated Twist, to the left Parivṛtta Sukhāsana

    • Return to center and turn left. Turn from your ribs, not by pulling with your arms.
    1:00

  20. Bound Angle Baddha Koṇāsana

    • Bring the soles of your feet together and let your knees open to the sides.
    • Slide a block under each thigh if your knees hover. Sit tall, or fold forward a little. You may notice a stretch along your inner thighs.
    2:30

  21. Bridge Pose Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana

    • Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart.
    • Inhale to lift your hips, exhale to lower. Three or four rounds, or rest your sacrum on a block for the whole hold.
    2:00

  22. Reclined Leg Stretch with a strap, right leg Supta Pādāṅguṣṭhāsana

    • Loop the strap around your right foot and lift the leg toward the ceiling.
    • Keep a soft bend in the knee. The left leg can stay bent with its foot on the floor.
    1:15

  23. Reclined Leg Stretch with a strap, left leg Supta Pādāṅguṣṭhāsana

    • Switch legs. Hold the strap with easy hands and keep both shoulders on the mat.
    1:15

  24. Knees to Chest Apānāsana

    • Hug both knees toward your chest. Rock gently side to side if you like.
    1:00

  25. Final Rest Śavāsana

    • Lie flat with a rolled blanket under your knees. Let your arms rest away from your sides, palms up.
    • Stay for the full seven minutes. If your mind wanders, return to the feeling of your breath.
    • When the timer ends, roll onto your side and use your hands to press up to sitting.
    7:00

Beginner modification path

Forty minutes with standing balances and floor work is a lot to take on at once. Every pose below has a chair or wall version that keeps the same shape and the same hold time. For any chair swap, use a sturdy chair without wheels, pushed against a wall so it cannot slide.

Chair and wall swaps for each pose in the Gentle Full Practice sequence
In the sequenceChair or wall swap
Easy Seat arrivalSit on the front half of a chair, feet flat and hip-width apart.
Cat-CowSeated Cat-Cow: hands on your knees, arch and round the spine while seated.
Child's Pose (poses 3 and 16)Sit on a chair and fold forward over a pillow on your lap, or rest your forearms on a table.
Downward-Facing DogHalf Dog at the wall: palms on the wall at hip height, walk back into an L shape.
Low Lunge (both sides)Standing lunge holding the back of a chair.
Standing Forward FoldHands on the chair seat with a long back, knees soft.
Mountain PoseStand with your back against a wall, or do the same arm sweeps seated.
Warrior II (both sides)Seated Warrior II: sit sideways on a chair with the front thigh on the seat and the back leg extended behind you, foot on the floor.
Extended Triangle (both sides)Stand with your back body close to a wall and rest the lower hand on a block or the chair seat.
Tree Pose (both sides)Stand side-on to a wall with one hand on it, or keep the lifted foot's toes on the floor.
Cobra PoseWall Cobra: palms on the wall at chest height, lift your chest and draw your shoulders back while standing.
Seated Forward BendSit on a chair, extend one leg with the heel on the floor, and hinge forward. Switch legs halfway.
Easy Seated Twist (both sides)Seated twist in a chair, knees facing forward.
Bound AngleSeated figure-four in a chair, switching sides halfway, or sit higher on two folded blankets.
Bridge PoseRest your sacrum on a block for the whole hold, or lie with knees bent and no lift.
Reclined Leg Stretch (both sides)Seated in a chair: extend one leg with the heel on the floor and loop the strap around that foot.
Knees to ChestHug one knee at a time, with the other foot on the floor.
Final RestLie with your calves on a chair seat, recline in a chair with your feet up, or rest on your side with a pillow between your knees.

Weeks one and two

  • Practice poses 1 to 14, from Easy Seat through Tree Pose. That takes eighteen and a half minutes.
  • Then pause the timer and lie down for a few minutes of rest on your own.

Weeks three and four

  • Practice all forty minutes, with as many swaps from the table as you like.
  • Pause whenever you need a breather. There is nothing to catch up on.

After that

  • Move one swap at a time to the floor version, if you want to.
  • Keep any swap that suits your body. Both paths are the full class.

Practice with care

This class is general movement education, not medical advice. Check with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition.

If you have high or low blood pressure, glaucoma or are prone to dizziness, use the wall version of Downward-Facing Dog and come up from forward folds slowly, keeping your head above your heart. Use the wall for Tree Pose if your balance is unsteady, and the wall or chair versions if you have a wrist, knee or low back injury. Stop any pose that causes pain, numbness or tingling, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. Read the Health & Safety Disclaimer.
