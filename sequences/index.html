Guided Sequences

Four complete practices, from an 8-minute chair routine to a 40-minute class. Each one gives you the poses in order, a hold time for every pose, a timer that moves you along, and a chair or wall version of every shape.

How a sequence differs from the pose library

The Pose Library teaches one shape at a time: how to get into it, where your hands and feet go, what you may notice, and how to come out. It is the place to slow down and study a single pose.

A sequence puts several of those shapes in an order, gives each one a hold time, and plans the transitions between them, so you can practice without stopping to decide what comes next. You follow the timer, and the thinking has already been done.

The order is deliberate. Every sequence opens with breath and gentle spinal movement, builds toward the larger shapes once your body has been moving for a few minutes, and ends with something quiet. Sides are always paired, right then left, with equal holds.

You do not need to read every pose guide before you start. Each pose in a sequence comes with two or three short cues, and wherever we have a full guide, the pose name links to it so you can check the details afterward.

On every sequence page

  • The poses in order, with English and Sanskrit names where an honest Sanskrit name exists
  • A hold time for each pose, adding up exactly to the stated length
  • A timer that counts each hold and moves you to the next pose
  • A chair, wall or prop swap for every pose, plus a short plan for building up
  • A print button that gives you a clean list for your mat

Choose a sequence

All four are written for beginners. Pick by the time you have, the space you are in and whether you want to stand, sit or lie down.

10-Minute Morning

Twelve poses that move your spine in every direction, wake up the legs and finish with two balance poses and a short rest.

10 min Beginner No props required

Desk Reset

Fourteen short poses done in a sturdy chair or standing beside it, with no mat and no floor work.

8 min Beginner Chair without wheels

Evening Wind-Down

Twelve slower poses for the end of the day, mostly on the floor, with long holds supported by blocks or pillows.

20 min Beginner 2 blocks or pillows, blanket

Gentle Full Practice

A complete class with breath, a warm-up, standing poses, a gentle backbend, seated stretches and a seven-minute rest.

40 min Beginner Mat, 2 blocks, strap, blanket

How the timer works

Each sequence page has a timer beside the pose list. It is silent, so set your screen where you can glance at it from your mat or chair.

  1. Start and pause. Press Start and the arch-shaped dial fills as the hold counts down. Press Pause at any point; the button changes to Resume and the hold picks up where it stopped.
  2. Each pose has its own hold. Holds are set pose by pose, not as one long countdown, so a 30-second side bend and a seven-minute rest live in the same timer. The current pose is highlighted in the list, and the timer shows what comes next and for how long.
  3. Previous and next. Use Previous to go back a pose and Next pose to skip ahead. Moving to a pose starts its hold from the beginning.
  4. The end. When the last hold finishes, a short note appears. Restart from pose 1 takes you back to the top whenever you like.

Printing a sequence

The Print this sequence button sends a clean version to your printer: the pose list with hold times, the swap table and the safety note. The site header, footer, photographs, timer and advertisements are left off the page.

Screen readers and motion settings

The timer announces each new pose to screen readers. If you have turned on your device's setting for less motion on screen, the dial updates without animation. And if JavaScript is switched off, the full pose list and every hold time still appear on the page.

Make any sequence yours

The written order is a starting point, not a rule. Each of these choices gives you the complete practice, adjusted for your body and your day.

Chair

The chair path

  • Every sequence has a chair swap for each pose in its modification table.
  • Use a sturdy chair without wheels, pushed against a wall so it cannot slide.
  • Follow the same timer and the same hold times.
Wall

The wall path

  • A clear stretch of wall turns Downward-Facing Dog into Half Dog.
  • It gives you a steady hand in Tree Pose and a reference line in Triangle.
  • It is also where Legs Up the Wall gets its name.
Pause

Pause as often as you like

  • The timer waits for you. Pause to adjust a prop, find a blanket or take a sip of water.
  • Rest in Child's Pose or in your chair, then press Resume.
  • Skipping a pose with Next pose is always allowed.
Repeat

Repeat a side

  • If one side felt different, press Previous to run that hold again.
  • Two sides rarely feel identical, and that is ordinary.
  • Notice the difference with curiosity rather than judgment.

Practice with care

These sequences are general movement education, not medical advice. Check with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition. Each sequence page has its own safety note at the bottom. Stop any pose that causes pain, numbness or tingling, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance.
