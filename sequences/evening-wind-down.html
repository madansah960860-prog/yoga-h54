Evening Wind-Down

A slower, floor-based sequence for the end of the day. Twelve poses, most of them on your back, with longer holds and fewer transitions than our other practices. Blocks or firm pillows carry much of your weight, so the work is mostly in arriving and staying put.

Start the timer
Total duration
20 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Poses
12
Props required
2 blocks or firm pillows, a blanket. Bolster and strap optional
i

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. Several poses ask you to lie on your back or rest your legs up a wall, so read the cautions at the bottom of this page and the Health & Safety Disclaimer before you begin.

Pose 1 of 12

Easy Seat breathing

Sukhāsana

2:00

Sit on a folded blanket so your knees are at or below your hips. Breathe through your nose. For the second minute, let each exhale run a count or two longer than the inhale.

Sequence complete. Roll onto your side and press up to sitting when you are ready.

Pause any time. If a pose does not feel right today, skip it or use the swap listed below.

Pose-by-pose order

Hold times add up to exactly twenty minutes. The first five poses move you gently; from pose 6 onward you are on your back, and the props do most of the holding.

i

Set up before you start

  • Lay your mat with one short end against a clear wall, so Legs Up the Wall is a short scoot away rather than a trip across the room.
  • Keep two blocks or two firm pillows and a blanket within reach. Add a bolster or a strap if you have them.
  • Have an extra layer handy. You will be still for several minutes at a time, and bodies cool down when they stop moving.

  1. Easy Seat breathing Sukhāsana

    • Sit on a folded blanket so your knees are at or below your hips, or sit on a chair with your feet flat.
    • Breathe through your nose. For the second minute, let each exhale run a count or two longer than the inhale.
    2:00

  2. Cat-Cow Mārjaryāsana-Bitilāsana

    • Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips, a blanket under your knees if you like.
    • Inhale to lift chest and tailbone; exhale to round. One slow, full breath for each direction.
    1:00

  3. Child's Pose Bālāsana

    • Knees wide, big toes touching, hips sinking toward your heels.
    • Rest your forehead on a block or a pillow. Arms reach forward or lie alongside your body.
    1:30

  4. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana

    • Step the right foot between your hands and lower the left knee onto a folded blanket.
    • Hands on blocks either side of your front foot. Let your hips ease forward until you notice the front of the left hip lengthen.
    1:00

  5. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana

    • Return to hands and knees, then step the left foot forward. Same padding, same slow pace.
    1:00

  6. Supported Bridge on a block Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana

    • Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart.
    • Lift your hips and slide a block under your sacrum, the flat bony area just below your waist. Start with the block on its lowest height.
    • Let your weight rest on the block. To come out, lift your hips, remove the block and lower slowly.
    2:00

  7. Reclined Bound Angle Supta Baddha Koṇāsana

    • Bring the soles of your feet together and let your knees fall open.
    • Slide a block or pillow under each thigh so your legs are fully supported. A bolster lengthwise under your spine is optional.
    • If you have a strap, a loop around your hips and feet keeps the feet from sliding away.
    2:30

  8. Reclined Twist, knees to the right Supta Matsyendrāsana

    • Bring your knees together and hug them in, then lower both knees to the right.
    • Rest them on a pillow if they hover. Arms open wide, both shoulders heavy on the mat.
    1:15

  9. Reclined Twist, knees to the left Supta Matsyendrāsana

    • Draw the knees back through center and lower them to the left.
    1:15

  10. Legs Up the Wall Viparīta Karaṇī

    • Sit sideways with one hip against the wall, then swing your legs up as you lower your back to the mat.
    • Your hips can sit a few inches from the wall and your knees can bend. A folded blanket under your hips is optional.
    2:30

  11. Knees to Chest Apānāsana

    • Bend your knees and roll to one side to come away from the wall.
    • Lie on your back and hug your knees toward your chest. Rock gently side to side if that feels good.
    1:00

  12. Final Rest Śavāsana

    • Lie flat with a pillow or rolled blanket under your knees and a blanket over you.
    • Let your arms rest a little away from your sides, palms up, and stay still for the full three minutes.
    • When the timer ends, roll onto your side and use your hands to press up to sitting.
    3:00

Beginner modification path

If getting down to the floor is not practical today, or one shape does not suit your body, use these swaps. Several happen in a chair or with your legs on a chair seat; they are complete versions of the same shapes, not consolation prizes. For any chair swap, use a sturdy chair without wheels, pushed against a wall so it cannot slide.

Chair, wall and pillow swaps for each pose in the Evening Wind-Down sequence
In the sequenceChair, wall or pillow swap
Easy Seat breathingSit on the front half of a chair, feet flat and hip-width apart.
Cat-CowSeated Cat-Cow: hands on your knees, arch and round the spine while seated.
Child's PoseSit on a chair and fold forward over a pillow on your lap, or rest your forearms and forehead on a table.
Low Lunge (both sides)Standing lunge holding the back of a chair, or a second folded blanket under the back knee if kneeling is the issue.
Supported BridgeReplace the block with a folded blanket, or rest with your knees bent and feet flat and no lift at all.
Reclined Bound AngleKeep your feet flat on the floor and let your knees lean in toward each other, or stack more pillows under each thigh.
Reclined Twist (both sides)Seated twist in a chair, or lower your knees only partway onto a stack of pillows.
Legs Up the WallLie on your back with your calves resting on the seat of a chair or sofa, knees bent at about a right angle.
Knees to ChestHug one knee at a time, with the other foot on the floor.
Final RestLie on your side with a pillow between your knees, or recline in a chair with your feet up.

Building into the full twenty minutes

  1. First few evenings: start at pose 6, Supported Bridge. Press Next pose five times to get there. Poses 6 to 12 take thirteen and a half minutes and never ask you to kneel.
  2. Next: add Easy Seat breathing, Cat-Cow and Child's Pose at the start, with any swap from the table. That makes eighteen minutes.
  3. Then: practice the full sequence, including both lunges. Keep any swap you prefer for as long as you like; both paths are the full practice.

Practice with care

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. Check with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition.

If you are pregnant, ask your healthcare provider about lying on your back and use the side-lying rest. If you have high or low blood pressure, glaucoma or are prone to dizziness, ask before practicing Legs Up the Wall, and come out of it slowly. Pad or skip the kneeling poses if your knees object. Stop any pose that causes pain, numbness or tingling, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. Read the Health & Safety Disclaimer.
