Your First Two Weeks on the Mat: A Gentle, Realistic Plan

Fourteen short sessions of 5 to 20 minutes, built from the sequences and pose guides on this site, with honest notes on what to expect and what to do when life gets in the way.

By Rosa Delgado Published 11/09/2026 7 min read

The first two weeks of a home practice are less about any single pose and more about building a routine you can actually keep. Plans that start too long or too rigid rarely survive ordinary life. This plan goes the other way: fourteen sessions, none longer than 20 minutes, built entirely from the sequences and pose guides already on this site.

You do not need any experience. You do need a patch of floor, a sturdy chair without wheels, a clear stretch of wall and, for a couple of sessions, two firm pillows or yoga blocks and a blanket. If you have not set up a space yet, Start Here walks through it, and Practice at Home Setup covers floors, lighting and storage.

Why short sessions come first

A 10-minute practice is not a watered-down version of a real one. It is long enough to move your spine forward, back and side to side, work your legs, try a balance and rest, and short enough to fit before work, at lunch or after dinner. Short sessions also tend to leave you willing to come back, which matters more in week one than any single hold.

Spreading new shapes across many short sessions, instead of packing them into one long one, also gives you time to get to know each pose before the next one arrives.

Week one: meeting the shapes

In the first week, the aim is to meet the main shapes once or twice each and learn where the supported versions live. Before each pose, read the "Choose your version" section of its guide and pick the version that looks most comfortable, not the one that looks most impressive.

  1. Day 1, 5 minutes. Read the Child's Pose guide and practice the pose for about a minute in two versions. Then sit and breathe slowly through your nose for 2 minutes.
  2. Day 2, 10 minutes. Follow the 10-Minute Morning with its on-page timer, using any chair or wall swap you like.
  3. Day 3, 8 minutes. Practice the Desk Reset, which happens entirely in a chair.
  4. Day 4, 10 minutes. Try Downward-Facing Dog and Cobra Pose in two versions each, holding 3 to 5 breaths, with Child's Pose in between.
  5. Day 5, 20 minutes. Follow the Evening Wind-Down, with blocks or firm pillows and a blanket nearby.
  6. Day 6, 10 minutes. Repeat the 10-Minute Morning.
  7. Day 7, rest. Take the day off, or spend 5 minutes with Bridge Pose and Child's Pose.

Week two: repeating and linking

Week two repeats what you met in week one and adds a little time and two new standing poses. Repetition is the point. The second time through a sequence, you spend less attention on what comes next and more on how each pose feels.

  1. Day 8, 10 minutes. The 10-Minute Morning. Notice one pose that feels more familiar than it did on Day 2.
  2. Day 9, 8 minutes. The Desk Reset, at your desk or kitchen table.
  3. Day 10, 15 minutes. Tree Pose beside a wall for 5 breaths on each side, then Extended Triangle Pose with your lower hand on a block or a chair seat.
  4. Day 11, 20 minutes. The Evening Wind-Down.
  5. Day 12, 15 minutes. The 10-Minute Morning, followed by three rounds of Bridge Pose, 5 breaths each.
  6. Day 13, rest. No practice needed.
  7. Day 14, up to 20 minutes. Your choice. Repeat your favorite session, or try the first 20 minutes of the Gentle Full Practice and stop there.

If a day says 15 minutes and you have 5, do 5. A few poses from the plan still count as practice.

What to expect in your body

Every body responds in its own way, but a few experiences come up often enough to be worth naming.

  • New sensations in familiar places. You may notice a stretch along the backs of your thighs in Downward-Facing Dog, or steady effort in the fronts of your thighs in Chair Pose. That is the pose asking those muscles to lengthen or to work.
  • Mild soreness a day or two later. As with any new activity, muscles you have not used this way may feel tender for a few days. Joint pain is a different matter and a reason to stop.
  • Wobbly balance. In Tree Pose your standing foot and ankle make constant small corrections. Wobbling is part of balancing, not a sign of doing it wrong, and a hand on the wall is a complete version.
  • Wrists and knees speaking up. Hands-and-knees poses load the wrists and knees. A folded blanket under the knees, or the wall version of Down Dog, changes how much they carry.

What you should not expect is a dramatic change in two weeks. This plan is about learning shapes and building a habit, not hitting a target.

Chair and wall versions are the plan, not plan B

Every pose guide here has a "Choose your version" section, and every sequence lists chair or wall swaps. Use them freely. A chair brings the floor up to seat height, so standing shapes become possible without balance demands or getting down to the ground. A wall gives you a fixed surface to press into or lean against, so you can feel where upright actually is.

Some readers practice the whole two weeks in a chair, especially if getting down to the floor is difficult. That is a complete practice. Others mix and match, and the choice can change from one day to the next.

The version that fits your body today is the whole pose. There is no lesser version to graduate from.

If you want to understand what each prop changes, Chair, Wall, Block, Strap: Why Props Are Equal Partners goes into the mechanics.

When you miss a day

You will miss days. Everyone does. When it happens, pick the plan up where you left off instead of doubling up to catch up. Two sessions squeezed into one day after a gap tend to feel rushed, and rushing is when people push past sensible limits.

If you miss several days in a row, go back two or three sessions and repeat them. If a full week slips by, start week one again; the shapes will already be familiar the second time through.

It also works to lower the bar. On a day when 10 minutes feels like too much, do one thing: a minute of Child's Pose, or five slow breaths in a chair. Small days keep the routine alive.

Tracking without pressure

Tracking is useful as long as it records what happened instead of judging it. Skip streak counters and targets. After each session, jot down three things on paper or in a notes app:

  1. The date and which session you did, even if you shortened it.
  2. One pose that felt good or interesting.
  3. One pose that felt hard, and which version you chose.

After two weeks you will have an honest record of what you practiced, and a ready list of questions if you decide to try a class with a teacher.

When to slow down

Slowing down is part of the plan, not a failure of it. Shorten a session, choose a more supported version or take an extra rest day if:

  • a pose causes sharp, pinching or electric sensations, especially in or near a joint;
  • you feel dizzy or lightheaded, particularly when rising from a fold or coming out of a pose with your head below your heart;
  • soreness builds from one session to the next instead of fading;
  • you catch yourself holding your breath to get through a pose.

Come out of any pose slowly, rest in a comfortable position, and either try a supported version another day or leave that pose out for now.

Check with a professional before you begin. This plan is general movement education, not medical advice. Talk with a qualified healthcare professional before starting, especially if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition, and stop if anything hurts.

After day fourteen

At the end of two weeks, look back at your notes. If the 10-Minute Morning feels comfortable, try the full 40-minute Gentle Full Practice once a week. If you enjoyed reading the pose guides, browse the Pose Library and add one new pose each week, starting with its most supported version. If phrases like "square your hips" still sound like a foreign language, How to Read a Yoga Cue translates the most common ones.

If you only managed half the sessions, you still practiced several times. Start the plan again and keep the versions that suit you.
