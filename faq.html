General education, not medical advice

Nothing on this page or this site is medical advice. If you are pregnant, injured or managing a health condition, talk with a qualified healthcare professional before you practice, and read the Health & Safety Disclaimer.

Getting started

Begin with the Start Here page. It walks you through setting up a small space, choosing your first few poses and following the 10-Minute Morning sequence, which needs no props. Before you start, read the Health & Safety Disclaimer, and check with a qualified healthcare professional first if you are pregnant, recently injured or managing a health condition.

No. Flexibility is not an entry requirement. Every pose guide includes chair, wall and block versions that bring the floor closer to you. Bent knees in a forward fold, or hands on blocks in Downward-Facing Dog, are complete versions of those poses, not lesser ones.

A non-slip surface and a clear patch of floor about as long as you are tall, with room to reach your arms out to the sides. A mat helps. A sturdy chair without wheels, a clear wall, two blocks (or firm pillows), a strap (or a belt) and a folded blanket cover nearly every version in our guides. The Practice at Home Setup page goes through each one.

As long as you have. Our guided sequences run 8, 10, 20 and 40 minutes. Ten minutes a few times a week is a realistic place to start, and Your First Two Weeks on the Mat lays out a gentle plan you can follow day by day.

Practice and safety

No. Our guides are general movement education. They describe how poses move the body, not what they do for any health condition. Our founder and lead teacher is a yoga teacher, not a doctor, physical therapist or clinician. For questions about your own health, talk with a qualified healthcare professional.

Stop and come out of it slowly. The feeling of effort or a stretch is different from sharp, pinching or burning sensations, or numbness or tingling, which are signals to stop. Try a supported version, or skip the pose for today. If pain continues after you practice, talk with a healthcare professional before you continue.

Talk with your healthcare provider before starting or continuing yoga during pregnancy, and tell any teacher you practice with. Our pose guides flag poses that need extra care during pregnancy and point you to a chair or wall version. A teacher with prenatal training can give you in-person guidance.

Please check with your healthcare provider first, and bring the specific poses you want to try. Every pose guide has a "Contraindications and care" block listing who should take care with the pose or avoid it. Those notes are cautions, not a complete list, and they cannot account for your history. A qualified teacher working with you in person can help you adapt.

Props and modifications

Yes. A chair, a wall, a block or a strap changes where the floor is, so the same shape becomes available on more days and to more bodies. We present every version as the pose, and many experienced students choose supported versions on purpose. Chair, Wall, Block, Strap: Why Props Are Equal Partners explains the mechanics.

Firm cushions or a stack of thick hardcover books can stand in for blocks in many seated and supported poses. A belt, a scarf or a robe tie works as a strap. Choose things that will not slide or collapse under your weight, and skip anything wobbly for poses where you lean on your hands.

A sturdy chair without wheels or arms and with a flat seat, ideally pushed against a wall so it cannot slide. Most chair versions, including the Desk Reset sequence, work at a seat height where your feet rest flat on the floor with your knees about level with your hips.

No. We do not rank brands or call any product the top choice. When a guide links to a retailer, the link is labeled as an affiliate link right where it appears. What matters more than the brand: a block that is firm and does not slide, a strap with a buckle or D-ring you can adjust easily, and a mat that grips your floor.

Ads, affiliates and sponsorships

Display advertising pays for the free pose library and sequences. Ads are labeled "Advertisement," kept away from instructions, buttons and the sequence timer, and left off legal pages. Personalized ads load only if you allow advertising cookies.

A link to a retailer that may pay us a small commission if you buy something, at no extra cost to you. Every affiliate link on this site is labeled right where it appears, and the full details are in our Advertising & Affiliate Disclosure.

Sponsored pages carry a "Sponsored" label at the very top, before the headline. In the newsletter, a sponsored section is labeled "Sponsored" in the email. Sponsors never review or edit our safety guidance.

No. Advertisers and sponsors do not choose topics for our guides, approve our cues or edit safety notes, and we decline any that make medical or health claims. Ad networks choose which display ads appear, so if you see one that makes health promises, please tell us through the contact page so we can block it.

Privacy and cookies

Essential storage that remembers your cookie choice and keeps forms working is always on. Analytics and advertising cookies are set only if you allow them in the cookie banner or the cookie preferences dialog. The Cookie Policy lists the details.

Use the "Cookie preferences" link in the footer of any page, or the button below these questions, to check and change your choices at any time.

We never sell your email address or contact details. Some advertising cookies may count as "sharing" under certain US state privacy laws, so you can opt out at any time with the "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link in the footer. We also honor Global Privacy Control signals from your browser.

Use the contact form and choose "Privacy request" as the topic, or use the privacy contact listed in our Privacy Policy. We will confirm that we received your request and respond within the time required by applicable law.

Teachers and directory

Teachers submit a listing with their city and state, formats, styles, a short description and their training. We confirm contact details and review each listing before publication. Listings are provided by the teachers themselves and are not endorsements. Browse the directory.

A paid placement that costs $19 per month. A featured listing appears first in its state and is always marked "Featured listing (paid placement)." It shows exactly the same information as a free listing.

We confirm that contact details work, but we cannot verify health or medical qualifications, and we do not certify teachers. Always ask a teacher directly about their training, their experience with students in your situation, and whether they carry professional liability insurance.

Use the List your teaching form on the Find a Teacher page. A basic listing is free. If you choose a featured listing, we email you a secure checkout link after your listing is approved; the form never asks for card details.
