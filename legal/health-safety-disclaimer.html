General movement education, not medical advice

!

All content on this site is general movement education. It is not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment, and it is not a substitute for care from a qualified healthcare professional.

That applies to everything we publish: pose guides, sequences, the sequence timer, the breath pacer, articles, the newsletter, membership downloads and digital products. Our content is written for a general adult audience. We do not know your medical history, your injuries, your medications or how your body feels today, so nothing here can be tailored to you.

Please do not ignore professional medical advice, or delay seeking it, because of something you read on this site.

Check with a healthcare professional first

!

Consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical, or managing a health condition.

It helps to be specific. Show your clinician the pose or sequence you are considering, and ask which movements, positions or breathing patterns you should avoid or change. Examples worth raising include heart or blood pressure conditions, glaucoma or other eye conditions, osteoporosis or low bone density, joint replacements, spinal conditions or recent spinal surgery, balance or inner-ear problems, and any condition that affects your breathing.

Have that conversation again whenever your situation changes, for example after a new diagnosis, a fall, a surgery or the start of a pregnancy.

Stop if something feels wrong

!

Stop immediately if you feel pain, dizziness, numbness, tingling, shortness of breath or any discomfort that seems wrong to you.

A stretch you can breathe through steadily is different from pain. Sharp, pinching, burning or electric sensations, pain inside a joint, and anything that makes you hold your breath are signals to come out of the pose slowly and rest. If a symptom does not settle when you rest, or returns each time you practice, see a healthcare professional before you continue.

You never need to finish a sequence. The sequence timer and the breath pacer can be paused or stopped at any point, and every pose has a resting alternative.

No instructor-student or provider-patient relationship

Reading the site, following a sequence, emailing us or buying a product does not create an instructor-student relationship, a provider-patient relationship or any other professional relationship between you and us, our founder or any teacher listed in our directory. We cannot see you practice, so we cannot correct your alignment, notice warning signs or adjust a pose for you in the moment.

For the same reason, we cannot answer individual health questions by email, such as whether a pose is safe with a particular condition. Those questions belong with your healthcare professional.

Work with a qualified teacher

!

For hands-on guidance and feedback on your alignment, work with a qualified teacher in person.

A good teacher can watch you move, offer versions that suit your body and help you build a practice gradually. Our Find a Teacher directory can help you look, but listings are not endorsements and we do not verify credentials. Ask any teacher about their training, their experience and how they adapt classes for people with health considerations, and tell them about anything your healthcare professional has advised.

No health claims

!

We make no medical, therapeutic or health claims about any pose, sequence or breathing technique.

Our guides describe what physically happens in a pose: which joints move, which muscles lengthen or work, where your weight goes and what you may notice. We do not claim that any pose, sequence or breathing pattern will produce a health outcome, and we suggest caution toward anyone who does. Your experience of practice is your own and may be quite different from someone else's.

Props and home practice safety

Most of our guides offer chair, wall and prop versions. Each is a complete way to practice, and each needs a safe setup. Our Practice at Home Setup and Props & Modifications guides go into more detail.

  • Chairs: use a stable chair without wheels, with all four legs on the floor and no arms in the way. Set it against a wall, or on a non-slip mat, so it cannot slide or tip. Avoid folding chairs that might collapse, rocking chairs and stools.
  • Walls: practice beside a solid, clear stretch of wall, away from shelves, frames, windows, radiators and doors that might open.
  • Floor: use a non-slip surface, such as a yoga mat on a hard floor. Avoid placing a mat on a loose rug or slippery boards, and practice barefoot or in grippy socks rather than ordinary socks.
  • Space: clear the area of furniture with sharp corners, cords, toys and anything you could trip over or knock into. Allow about the length of a mat, plus an arm's reach on each side and overhead.
  • Blocks, straps, blankets and bolsters: check props for wear, set blocks on a stable surface and never wrap a strap around your neck or fasten it so that you cannot release it quickly.
  • Getting down and up: if moving to and from the floor is hard, practice the chair versions, or keep a sturdy chair beside you to hold as you move.

Practice when you are not rushed, distracted or very tired, and keep a phone within reach if you practice alone.

Breathwork cautions

!

Do not practice breath retention (holding the breath in or out) if you are pregnant or have a heart or blood pressure condition. Stop any breathing exercise if you feel lightheaded, dizzy or short of breath, and return to your normal breathing.

Our breath pacer and Breathwork Basics guide use slow, comfortable counts. Never force a count; shorten it until it feels easy. Patterns that include holds are optional, and you can always choose one without them. Avoid rapid or forceful breathing techniques unless a qualified teacher has shown you how and your healthcare professional agrees they suit you. If you have asthma or another condition that affects your breathing, ask your clinician before you begin. Practice breathwork sitting or lying down, and never while driving, in water or anywhere a dizzy spell could be dangerous.

Pregnancy and postpartum

Our guides are not written specifically for pregnancy or the postpartum period. If you are pregnant, check with your obstetric provider or midwife before using any of our content, and consider a teacher trained in prenatal yoga. Pregnant practitioners are often advised to avoid deep twists, lying on the belly, long periods lying flat on the back later in pregnancy, breath retention, overheating and pushing toward the end of their range, but your own provider's advice always comes first. After giving birth, including after a cesarean birth, wait until your provider has cleared you for exercise, then return gradually.

Older adults and mobility

Our chair and wall versions are complete poses, not lesser ones, and many readers use them every day. If you are an older adult, have limited mobility, use a mobility aid, or have a history of falls, osteoporosis or joint replacement, check with your healthcare professional about which movements are appropriate for you. Choose supported versions of balance poses, keep a chair or wall within reach, move slowly between standing, sitting and lying down, and pause for a few breaths before you stand up after resting or folding forward.

Children and teens

Our content is written for adults. It is not designed for children, and the site is not directed to children under 13. Teenagers aged 13 to 17 should practice only with a parent's or guardian's involvement. Children who practice yoga should do so with a teacher trained to work with children, and with an adult supervising.

Assumption of risk

Yoga, breathwork and other physical activity involve inherent risks, including strains, sprains, falls and, rarely, serious injury. By using this site and its content, you voluntarily accept those risks. You are responsible for deciding whether to practice, choosing appropriate versions, setting up a safe space and listening to your body. To the fullest extent the law allows, we are not liable for any injury, loss or damage arising from your use of our content, as our Terms of Use explain in more detail.

In an emergency

!

If you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, sudden weakness or numbness, or any other symptom that could be an emergency, stop and call 911 right away, or your local emergency number outside the United States.

Do not wait to finish a practice, and do not email us about an urgent health concern; we cannot respond in time and cannot give medical advice. For non-urgent questions about this disclaimer, write to hello@longshadowyoga.us or use our Contact page.
