Accessibility Statement

Last updated 11/16/2026

We want every reader to be able to use our guides, sequences and practice tools, whatever device, browser or assistive technology they rely on. Here is what we have done, what we know is not right yet, and how to tell us.

On this page

Our commitment

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA across this website. WCAG is the internationally recognized standard for accessible web content, published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). Accessibility is part of how we design and build every page, not a feature added at the end.

It shapes our writing too. We use plain language and numbered steps, we describe what a pose feels like as well as how it looks, and we present chair, wall and prop versions as complete options so that the guides work for varied bodies, ages and levels of mobility.

Based on our own review, we consider the site partially conformant with WCAG 2.1 Level AA. That means most content meets the standard, and the parts we know do not yet fully meet it are listed under Known limitations.

Measures we take

  • Structure and landmarks: every page uses semantic regions for the header, navigation, main content and footer, has one main heading and keeps headings in a logical order, so screen reader users can move around quickly.
  • Skip link: the first item on every page is a "Skip to main content" link, which becomes visible when it receives keyboard focus.
  • Keyboard navigation: every link, menu, button, form, filter, accordion, the cookie preferences dialog, the sequence timer and the breath pacer can be used with a keyboard alone. Open menus close with the Escape key, and focus returns to the control that opened them.
  • Visible focus: a clear, high-contrast outline shows which element has keyboard focus.
  • Text alternatives: every informative photograph has alt text describing what it shows. Decorative shapes are hidden from assistive technology, and we never put essential text inside images.
  • Color contrast: body text and controls meet a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against their background. We do not rely on color alone to convey meaning; labels such as "Advertisement" and "Featured listing (paid placement)" are written out in words.
  • Reduced motion: if your device is set to reduce motion, we switch off scroll animations, the arch reveal effect and the timer and pacer animations, and pages appear without movement.
  • Forms: every field has a visible label. Errors appear as text beside the field and are linked to it so screen readers announce them, and focus moves to the first field that needs attention. Required fields are marked.
  • Resizable text: text is sized in relative units and layouts reflow, so you can zoom to 200% without losing content and, on most pages, without scrolling sideways.
  • Audio and video: nothing on the site plays audio automatically. If we add video in the future, each video will have accurate captions and a text transcript, and any audio-only content will have a transcript.
  • Language: pages declare English as their language, and Sanskrit pose names are marked with their own language code and always appear beside the English name.
  • Tables: data tables use header cells, so screen readers announce which row and column each value belongs to.

The sequence timer and breath pacer

Our two interactive practice tools were built to be followed without needing to watch the screen.

Sequence timer

  • The timer never starts on its own. It starts only when you press Start, and you can pause, resume, go back, skip to the next pose or restart from the first pose at any time, using standard buttons.
  • The Start button tells assistive technology whether the timer is running.
  • When the timer moves to a new pose, a polite live region announces the pose name and how long it lasts, without interrupting what your screen reader is already saying.
  • The time remaining is shown as text, and the full sequence is written out on the same page as a numbered list with hold times and cues, so you can practice at your own pace without the timer, or print it.

Breath pacer

  • The pacer starts only when you press Start pacing and stops when you press Stop, and its button state is announced.
  • Each phase, such as "Breathe in," "Hold" and "Breathe out," appears as text and is announced through a polite live region. The second-by-second count is visual only, so screen readers are not flooded with numbers.
  • Breathing patterns are chosen with labeled radio buttons inside a named group, and each pattern is also described in words in the surrounding guide.
  • Under reduced motion settings, the rising and falling fill is switched off and the text prompts remain.

If you use either tool with a screen reader or switch access and find the announcements too frequent, too sparse or confusing, please tell us. We would like to hear from you.

Known limitations

  • Third-party advertising: ads are supplied by outside ad networks, and their content may not fully conform to WCAG 2.1 AA. Each ad space is labeled and has reserved space so it does not shift the page. If an ad gets in your way, tell us where you saw it.
  • Embedded and linked content: websites we link to, such as retailers and teachers' own sites, and any future embeds, such as maps or video players, are outside our control and may not fully conform.
  • Downloadable PDFs: sequence PDFs and printable pose cards are designed for printing and may not be fully tagged for screen readers. Every sequence is also available as an accessible web page, and we will send an accessible format on request.
  • Sanskrit pronunciation: screen readers may mispronounce Sanskrit names with diacritics. The English name always appears alongside.

Compatibility

The site is designed to work with recent versions of these browsers and assistive technologies:

  • Browsers: Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge, on desktop and mobile.
  • Screen readers: VoiceOver on macOS and iOS, and NVDA with Firefox or Chrome on Windows.
  • Settings: browser zoom and text size, and your device's reduced motion preference.

The site relies on HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Guides, pose descriptions and sequence lists can be read without JavaScript; interactive features, such as the timer, the pacer, filters and form checks, need it. Older browsers that are no longer updated, such as Internet Explorer, are not supported.

Feedback and contact

If you have trouble using any part of the site, or you need content in a different format, please tell us. It helps to include the page address, what you were trying to do and, if you are comfortable sharing it, the browser and assistive technology you use.

  • Online: the form on our Contact page

We aim to respond within 5 business days. If a page or file is not accessible to you, we will work with you to provide the information another way, such as plain text by email, while we fix the problem.

Formal complaints

If you are not satisfied with our response, reply with "Formal accessibility complaint" in the subject line. Our founder will review it personally and send you a written reply within 10 business days, explaining what we will change and when. You also have the right to contact the Civil Rights Division of the US Department of Justice, which enforces the Americans with Disabilities Act, or your state's civil rights or consumer protection office.

Assessment and review

This statement was prepared on 11/16/2026. It is based on a self-assessment of the site's page templates and key pages by our own team, using automated testing tools, keyboard-only testing and manual checks with VoiceOver and NVDA. We review this statement at least once a year and whenever we make significant changes to the site. Related reading: our Privacy Policy and Terms of Use.
