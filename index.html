Every version is the pose

A chair, a wall, a block or a strap is not a lesser way to practice. Each one changes where the floor is, so the same shape becomes available to more bodies on more days.

Chair

Chair

  • Brings the floor up to seat height
  • Lets you work standing shapes while seated
  • Steady support for balance poses
Wall

Wall

  • Gives your hands or back a fixed reference
  • Shows you where "straight" actually is
  • Takes weight off the wrists in inversions
Block

Block

  • Lifts the floor to meet your hand
  • Three heights: 4, 6 or 9 inches
  • Supports hips in seated and resting poses
Strap

Strap

  • Lengthens your arms when hands cannot meet
  • Keeps the spine long in forward bends
  • An 8-foot strap covers most uses

Every pose, broken down the same way

Entry steps, alignment cues, breath, hold time, common mistakes and supported versions, in the same order every time, so you always know where to look.

Tree Pose

Vṛkṣāsana
Beginner Balance Wall or chair optional

Entry steps

  1. Stand in Mountain Pose with your weight even across both feet.
  2. Shift your weight into your left foot and soften the left knee slightly.
  3. Turn your right knee out and place the right sole on the left ankle, calf or inner thigh. Avoid the knee joint itself.
  4. Bring your palms together at your chest, or rest your hands on your hips.

Alignment cues

  • Press the foot and the standing leg into each other with equal effort.
  • Keep both hip points facing forward, like headlights.
  • Let the tailbone drop toward the floor so the low back stays long.
  • Rest your gaze on one point that is not moving.

Breath and hold

  • Breathe slowly through the nose, letting the ribs widen on each inhale.
  • Hold for 5 to 8 breaths, about 30 to 45 seconds, on each side.

Common mistakes

  • Pressing the foot directly into the side of the knee.
  • Letting the standing hip jut out to the side.
  • Holding the breath to stay balanced.

Supported versions

  • Wall: stand side-on to a wall and rest one hand on it for steady contact.
  • Chair: hold the back of a sturdy chair, or sit and turn one knee out with the foot resting on the opposite ankle.
  • Kickstand: keep the toes of the lifted foot on the floor beside the standing heel.

Approach with care

Use a wall or chair if you are prone to dizziness, have a recent ankle, knee or hip injury, or are pregnant. Stop if you feel pain. This is general education; a qualified teacher can give you hands-on guidance. Read the Health & Safety Disclaimer.
