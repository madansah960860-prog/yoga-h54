Find a Teacher

Guides and sequences can take you a long way, but nothing replaces a teacher who can see you move. This directory lists teachers who teach in person or online, filterable by state, format and style, with every paid placement clearly marked.

Browse listings List your teaching

How the directory works

We built the directory the way we would want one to work as students: plain about what it is, plain about who paid for what.

  • Listings are free. Any teacher can request a listing at no cost. It shows their city and state, the formats and styles they teach, a short description, their training and a contact link.
  • Featured listings are paid placements. For $19 a month, a teacher's listing appears first in its state. It is always labeled "Featured listing (paid placement)," and it shows exactly the same information as a free listing.
  • Listings are not endorsements. Teachers write and submit their own listings. Being listed, or featured, does not mean we recommend a teacher.
  • What we check, and what we cannot. We confirm contact details and review every listing before publication, and we remove health claims. We cannot verify health or medical qualifications.
  • Always ask. Before you book, ask a teacher directly about their training and their experience with students like you. The questions further down this page are a good start.

What to look for in a teacher

The right teacher for you is the one who can see what your body is doing today and offer a version of each pose that works for it. A listing can tell you where someone teaches. These questions tell you how.

Most good teachers are happy to answer them by email before your first class.

Questions to ask first

  • What training have you completed, and how long have you been teaching?
  • What does a typical class look like from start to finish?
  • How do you offer options when a pose does not suit a student?
  • Do you use hands-on adjustments, and do you always ask first?

Experience with your situation

If you are pregnant, recovering from an injury, managing a health condition or new to exercise, ask whether the teacher has worked with students in a similar situation, and check with your healthcare provider before you start. A good teacher welcomes the question and will tell you plainly if a class is not a fit.

Class size

Smaller groups leave more room for individual attention. For your first few classes, a group of about ten or fewer, or a private session, makes it easier for a teacher to notice what you need and to set you up with props.

Chair and wall options

Ask whether chairs, blocks, straps and wall space are available, and whether students are encouraged to use them. In a class where props are equal partners, you will see experienced students reaching for them too.

Insurance and practical details

Ask whether the teacher carries professional liability insurance. For in-person classes, ask about step-free access, a place to sit, and the cancellation policy. For online classes, ask whether the teacher can see students on camera and offers feedback as you go.
