Cobra Pose

Bhujaṅgāsana

A low backbend from your belly: legs long behind you, hands under your shoulders, chest lifting forward and up. The muscles along your back do most of the lifting while the front of your chest and abdomen lengthen. Kept low, it is one of the gentlest ways to explore bending the spine backward.

Beginner Gentle backbend Blanket, wall or chair optional
Level
Beginner
Suggested hold
3 to 5 breaths, about 20 seconds
Body area
Spine, chest, abdomen, front of hips
Props
Optional: blanket, wall or chair

What happens in your body

Cobra is spinal extension that begins on the floor. Your back muscles lift your chest, your hands steady the lift rather than push it, and your legs stay long and active behind you. Here is what lengthens, what works and what moves.

Lengthens

  • Abdominal muscles along the front of the torso
  • Chest muscles (pectorals) and the front of the shoulders
  • Hip flexors at the front of the hips, gently

Works

  • Spinal extensors (erector spinae) along the length of the back
  • Muscles between the shoulder blades (rhomboids and middle trapezius)
  • Glutes and hamstrings, lightly, keeping the legs long
  • Triceps, lightly, as the hands steady the lift

Joints in motion

  • Spine extends; the neck and low back bend most readily
  • Elbows stay bent, close to the ribs
  • Shoulder blades draw back and down
  • Hips extend as the legs press down
  • Ankles point, tops of the feet on the mat

Pose breakdown

Bhujaṅgāsana
Beginner Hold 20 to 30 seconds

Entry steps

  1. Lie on your belly with your legs extended behind you, hip-width apart, and the tops of your feet on the mat.
  2. Place your hands flat under your shoulders, fingers spread and pointing forward.
  3. Draw your elbows in toward your ribs and rest your forehead on the mat.
  4. Press the tops of your feet and your pubic bone gently into the floor.
  5. On an inhale, lift your head and chest using the muscles of your back. Press only lightly into your hands.
  6. Rise only as high as feels comfortable, elbows still bent. Keep the back of your neck long, with your gaze on the floor a little ahead of you.
  7. On an exhale, lower slowly, chest and then forehead to the mat, and turn your head to one side to rest.

Alignment cues

  • Keep your elbows bent and hugging your sides, not winging out.
  • Widen across your collarbones and draw your shoulders away from your ears.
  • Keep your legs active: press all ten toenails into the mat without gripping your buttocks hard.
  • Reach your tailbone toward your heels so the bend spreads along your spine rather than collecting in your low back.
  • Lead with your breastbone moving forward and up, not your chin.
  • Test the lift: hover your hands off the mat for one breath. The height you can hold without them is your back muscles' share.

Breath pattern

  • Inhale as you lift.
  • Breathe slowly through the nose while you hold, and notice your belly press into the mat on each inhale.
  • Exhale as you lower.
  • If your breath becomes short or strained, come down a little lower.

Hold duration

3 to 5 breaths about 20 to 30 seconds

  • New to the pose: 2 or 3 breaths, then lower, rest and repeat 2 or 3 times.
  • With regular practice: build toward 5 to 8 breaths.
  • In a flowing sequence: often a single inhale before pressing back to Child's Pose or Downward-Facing Dog.

Common mistakes

  • Straightening the arms and pushing up with the hands, so the bend crowds into the low back.
  • Shoulders hunching up toward the ears.
  • Throwing the head back to look at the ceiling.
  • Elbows flaring out to the sides.
  • Squeezing the buttocks as hard as possible.
  • Holding the breath at the top.

Progression

  • Hands-free Cobra: hover your palms just off the mat for 2 or 3 breaths so your back muscles carry the whole lift.
  • A little higher: as the lift steadies, rise a little higher with your elbows still bent and your shoulders still low.
  • Toward Upward-Facing Dog (Ūrdhva Mukha Śvānāsana): an intermediate backbend with straight arms and the thighs lifted, worth learning with a teacher in person.
  • Longer holds: add one breath per week, from 3 breaths toward 8.

Contraindications and care

  • Ask your healthcare provider first if you have a back injury or a diagnosed spinal condition such as spinal stenosis or spondylolisthesis, and keep the lift very low or leave backbends out for now.
  • Avoid lying on your belly during pregnancy, especially as it progresses. The standing wall version or the seated chair version keeps the shape with no pressure on your abdomen.
  • After recent abdominal surgery, or if you have a hernia, wait for clearance from your healthcare provider.
  • Use the forearm or wall version if you have a wrist, elbow or shoulder injury, or carpal tunnel symptoms.
  • Keep your gaze down and the back of your neck long if you have a neck injury.
  • Stop if you feel pain, numbness or tingling. Work with a qualified teacher in person for hands-on guidance on your alignment.

Choose your version

Each of these is Cobra Pose. They keep the same lifted breastbone and gentle spinal extension while changing whether you stand, sit or lie down, and how much your wrists and belly carry.

Wall

Standing Cobra

  • Stand facing a wall about a foot away, palms on the wall at chest height, elbows bent close to your ribs.
  • On an inhale, lift your breastbone forward and up and draw your shoulders back and down.
  • Let your hips move slightly toward the wall and keep the back of your neck long.
  • No floor and no weight on the belly. The back of a sturdy chair works the same way.
Chair

Seated Cobra

  • Sit toward the front edge of a sturdy chair with your feet flat and hip-width apart.
  • Hold the sides of the seat beside or slightly behind your hips.
  • On an inhale, lift your breastbone and draw your shoulder blades toward each other and down.
  • The same spinal extension, from a seated base.
Sphinx

Low Cobra on forearms

  • Lie on your belly and place your forearms on the mat, parallel, palms down. This shape is often called Sphinx.
  • Set your elbows slightly in front of your shoulders for a gentler bend, or directly under them for more.
  • Press your forearms lightly into the mat and lift your chest.
  • Takes the load off your wrists, and steady enough to hold for 1 to 2 minutes.
Blanket

Blanket under the hips

  • Fold a blanket into a long rectangle and lie with it under the front of your hips and pelvis.
  • Cushions your hip bones and pubic bone on a firm floor.
  • Add a thin roll under your ankles if the tops of your feet feel pressure.
  • Practice Low Cobra or full Cobra on top of it exactly as described above.

Where this pose shows up

Cobra Pose belongs to our 40-minute Gentle Full Practice, and the Evening Wind-Down is a slower floor practice to pair it with. It also sits naturally beside the poses below.

Gentle Full Practice

Beginner, 40 minutes. A complete, unhurried class you can follow at home.

Evening Wind-Down

Beginner, 20 minutes. A slow, floor-based practice with blocks or firm pillows and a blanket.

Related poses

  • Bridge Pose (Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana), another gentle backbend, practiced from your back
  • Child's Pose (Bālāsana), the classic rest after Cobra, rounding the spine the other way
  • Downward-Facing Dog (Adho Mukha Śvānāsana), which often follows Cobra in flowing classes
