Bridge Pose

Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana

A gentle backbend you do lying on your back: feet planted, hips lifted, arms resting along the mat. The back of your hips and thighs do the lifting while the front of your hips and chest lengthen, and a single block turns it into a pose you can rest in.

Beginner Gentle backbend Block, chair or wall optional
Level
Beginner
Suggested hold
5 breaths, about 30 seconds
Body area
Glutes, hamstrings, front of hips, upper back
Props
Optional: block, chair or wall

What happens in your body

Bridge is a hip extension with your shoulders and feet as the base. The muscles at the back of your hips and thighs lift your pelvis, and your spine extends gently along its length. Here is what lengthens, what works and what moves.

Lengthens

  • Hip flexors at the front of the hips (iliopsoas and rectus femoris)
  • Quadriceps along the front of the thighs
  • Chest and the front of the shoulders
  • Abdominal muscles along the front of the torso

Works

  • Gluteus maximus, the main muscle lifting the hips
  • Hamstrings, which assist the glutes
  • Spinal extensors (erector spinae) along the back
  • Inner thighs (adductors), keeping the knees in line with the hips

Joints in motion

  • Hips extend as the pelvis lifts
  • Spine extends gently along its length
  • Shoulders extend as the arms press into the floor
  • Knees bend to roughly a right angle
  • Neck flexes, chin a little closer to the chest

Pose breakdown

Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana
Beginner Hold 30 to 60 seconds

Entry steps

  1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart and parallel.
  2. Walk your heels toward your hips until your fingertips can just brush them when your arms rest by your sides.
  3. Rest your arms along the mat, palms down. Look straight up at the ceiling and keep your head still from here on.
  4. On an inhale, press down through your feet and lift your hips off the mat, letting your spine peel up from the tailbone.
  5. Lift only as high as feels steady. Your weight rests across your upper back and shoulders, never on your neck.
  6. To come down, exhale and lower slowly: upper back first, then middle back, low back and finally your hips.

Alignment cues

  • Keep your knees pointing straight ahead, in line with your hips and ankles.
  • Press evenly through both feet, especially the big-toe side, so the knees do not drift apart.
  • Lift by pressing your feet down, not by pushing your belly toward the ceiling.
  • Press your upper arms into the mat and let your shoulder blades draw gently toward each other underneath you.
  • Leave a small space between the back of your neck and the mat.
  • Keep your jaw and throat soft.

Breath pattern

  • Breathe slowly in and out through the nose.
  • Inhale to lift your hips; exhale to lower them.
  • While you hold, notice your front ribs and belly widen a little on each inhale.
  • If you catch yourself holding your breath, lower your hips a little or come down and rest.

Hold duration

5 breaths about 30 seconds

  • New to the pose: 3 to 5 breaths, then lower and repeat 2 or 3 times.
  • With regular practice: build toward 1 minute.
  • Supported Bridge on a block: often 1 to 3 minutes, because the block carries your weight.

Common mistakes

  • Knees falling outward, or squeezing together, instead of staying hip-width apart.
  • Pushing the belly up to get higher, so the bend crowds into the low back.
  • Turning the head to look around while the hips are lifted, which loads the neck.
  • Feet so far from the hips that the hamstrings cramp, or so close that the heels lift.
  • Holding the breath to stay up.

Progression

  • Hands clasped underneath: interlace your fingers under your back, walk your shoulders slightly toward each other, then press your arms into the mat. Leave this out if it strains your shoulders.
  • One-Legged Bridge: from a steady Bridge, shift your weight into your left foot and extend your right leg in line with your thigh, hips level. Hold 3 breaths and switch.
  • Longer holds: once 5 breaths feel steady, add one breath per week up to 1 minute.

Contraindications and care

  • Avoid the full pose, or keep the lift low on a block, if you have a neck injury or neck pain. Bridge places weight across your shoulders with your neck flexed.
  • Ask your healthcare provider first if you have a recent back, knee or shoulder injury or a diagnosed spinal condition, and keep the lift small.
  • Keep the lift low and the hold short if you have high blood pressure, glaucoma or a tendency toward dizziness, since your hips rise above your heart.
  • During pregnancy, lying flat on your back may not be advised, especially later on. Ask your healthcare provider first.
  • Stop if you feel pain, numbness or tingling. Work with a qualified teacher in person for hands-on guidance on your alignment.

Choose your version

Each of these is Bridge Pose. They keep the same lifted hips and long front body while changing how far your spine bends and how much work the back of your legs does.

Block

Supported Bridge

  • Lift your hips and slide a block under your sacrum, the flat bony area at the base of your spine, not under your low back.
  • Start with the block on its lowest height, about 4 inches.
  • Let your hips rest their full weight on the block and your arms rest by your sides.
  • To come out, lift your hips, slide the block away and lower slowly.
Chair

Calves on a chair

  • Brace a sturdy chair against a wall and lie on your back in front of it.
  • Rest your calves on the seat so your knees bend at about a right angle.
  • Press the backs of your calves down into the seat and lift your hips a few inches.
  • Keeps the bend mostly in your hips, with your lower legs fully supported.
Wall

Feet on the wall

  • Lie on your back with your knees bent and the soles of your feet flat on a wall, shins roughly parallel to the floor.
  • Press your feet into the wall and lift your hips.
  • The wall gives your feet a surface that cannot slide away.
  • Walk your feet higher or lower on the wall to change the angle.
Flow

Moving Bridge

  • Set up exactly as for the full pose, feet planted and arms long.
  • Inhale to lift your hips; exhale to lower them, one section of your spine at a time.
  • Repeat 5 to 8 times at the pace of your breath.
  • A way to explore the shape before, or instead of, a longer hold.
