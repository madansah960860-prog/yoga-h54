Pose Library

How to use this library

Each card gives a pose's English and Sanskrit names, a short description of the shape, its level, the body areas it works or stretches, and the props it works with. Six poses link to a full step-by-step guide. The rest have quick cues you can open right on the card: how to come in, one key alignment point, and one note on care.

  • Beginner No previous yoga needed. These shapes are well supported, and each one has a chair, wall or floor-based alternative.
  • Intermediate Asks more of your wrists, balance or range of motion. Try these once the related beginner poses feel familiar.
  • Advanced Deeper or less stable shapes. Learn these from a qualified teacher in person before practicing them on your own.

What the prop tags mean

  • Optional: the pose works on a bare mat, and the prop is one of its equal, complete versions.
  • Recommended: most bodies will find the shape more available with the prop, so set it out before you start.
  • Required: the version on the card needs that prop to work as written, like the wall in Legs Up the Wall or the strap in Reclined Hand-to-Big-Toe Pose.
  • No props filter: shows the poses you can practice with nothing but a mat or a rug that does not slide.

Every pose here has a chair, wall or prop option. Those versions are the pose, not a lesser stand-in for it, and many experienced students use them every day.

Navāsana

Boat Pose

Navāsana

A seated balance on the sitting bones with the legs lifted and the torso leaning back, so the body makes a V. The abdominal muscles and hip flexors work to hold it.

Quick cues
  • Sit with your knees bent and feet flat, hold the backs of your thighs, and lean back until you balance just behind your sitting bones.
  • Lift your chest and keep your spine long. Raise your shins parallel to the floor before you try straightening your legs, or loop a strap around your feet.
  • Keep your toes on the floor if your lower back rounds or strains, and skip it during pregnancy or after recent abdominal surgery.
Advanced Core Strap optional
Baddha Koṇāsana

Bound Angle Pose

Baddha Koṇāsana

A seated pose with the soles of the feet together and the knees falling open to the sides. You may notice a stretch along the inner thighs.

Quick cues
  • Sit on a folded blanket, bring the soles of your feet together and let your knees open out to the sides.
  • Hold your ankles or shins and lengthen up through the crown of your head before leaning forward at all.
  • Never press your knees down with your hands. Rest blocks under your outer thighs if your knees sit high or your inner knees feel strained.
Beginner Hips Blanket or blocks optional

Bridge Pose

Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana

Lying on your back with knees bent and feet planted, you lift your hips toward the ceiling. The buttocks and backs of the thighs work while the fronts of the hips and chest open.

Beginner Legs Chest Block optional Full guide
Uṣṭrāsana

Camel Pose

Uṣṭrāsana

A kneeling backbend: the hips stay over the knees while the chest lifts and the hands reach back toward the heels. The fronts of the hips, thighs and chest lengthen.

Quick cues
  • Kneel with your knees hip-width apart on a folded blanket, place your hands on your lower back with fingers pointing down, and lift your chest before you lean back.
  • Keep your hips stacked over your knees, and reach for your heels, or for blocks beside your ankles, only if your chest stays lifted.
  • Keep your chin slightly tucked, come up slowly by leading with your chest, and avoid this pose with a recent neck or lower back injury.
Advanced Chest Spine Blocks or blanket optional

Cat-Cow

Mārjaryāsana-Bitilāsana

Two shapes linked with the breath on hands and knees: the back arches as the chest lifts, then rounds up toward the ceiling. Each section of the spine moves in turn.

Quick cues
  • Come to hands and knees with your wrists under your shoulders and knees under your hips, with a blanket under your knees if you like.
  • Inhale to tip your tailbone up and draw your chest forward. Exhale to tuck your tailbone and round from the lower back up to the neck.
  • Keep the movement small and smooth with a back or neck injury, and try it seated on a chair, hands on your knees, if your wrists or knees object.
Beginner Spine Blanket or chair optional

Chair Pose

Utkaṭāsana

A standing squat with the hips sitting back as if toward a chair and the arms reaching up. The fronts of the thighs and the buttocks work steadily.

Quick cues
  • Stand with your feet hip-width apart, bend your knees and sit your hips back, then reach your arms forward or overhead.
  • Keep your knees tracking over your second toes and your weight toward your heels, so you could wiggle your toes.
  • Hover just above a real chair seat, or keep your hands on your hips if your shoulders are tender. Come up if your knees ache.
Beginner Legs Core Chair or wall optional

Child's Pose

Bālāsana

A kneeling rest with the hips sinking toward the heels and the forehead resting down. The back of the body lengthens gently while very little has to work.

Beginner Hips Spine Blanket, bolster or block optional Full guide

Cobra Pose

Bhujaṅgāsana

A gentle face-down backbend: the legs stay long on the floor while the chest lifts, hands under the shoulders and elbows bent. The muscles along the spine do most of the lifting.

Beginner Spine Chest Blanket optional Full guide

Corpse Pose

Śavāsana

Lying still on your back with your arms and legs relaxed, usually at the end of a practice. The floor supports your whole body, so nothing has to hold you up.

Quick cues
  • Lie on your back, let your feet fall open, and rest your arms a little away from your sides with palms up or down.
  • Adjust until nothing needs effort. A rolled blanket or bolster under your knees takes pressure off the lower back for many people.
  • Later in pregnancy, or whenever lying flat is uncomfortable, rest on your side with a pillow between your knees instead.
Beginner Spine Blanket or bolster optional
Gomukhāsana

Cow Face Pose

Gomukhāsana

A seated pose with one knee stacked over the other and the hands reaching to meet behind the back. It asks for rotation at both the hips and the shoulders.

Quick cues
  • Sit on a folded blanket and stack one knee directly over the other with your feet beside your hips, or simply sit cross-legged.
  • Reach one arm up and bend it so the hand slides down your upper back, then bring the other hand behind you from below. Hold a strap between your hands if they do not meet.
  • Keep your ribs from flaring forward, and ease off if you feel pinching at the front of the shoulder or strain in either knee.
Intermediate Shoulders Hips Strap or blanket optional

Downward-Facing Dog

Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

An upside-down V with hands and feet on the mat and hips high. The backs of the legs lengthen while the shoulders hold the arms overhead.

Beginner Hamstrings Shoulders Blocks, wall or chair optional Full guide
Garuḍāsana

Eagle Pose

Garuḍāsana

A standing balance with one thigh wrapped over the other and the arms wound together in front of the face. You may notice a stretch across the upper back and the outer hip.

Quick cues
  • Bend both knees, cross one thigh over the other, and hook that foot behind the standing calf or rest its toes on the floor as a kickstand.
  • Wrap one arm under the other at the elbows and bring the palms or backs of the hands together, or simply hug your shoulders, lifting your elbows to shoulder height.
  • Stand with your back near a wall, or practice seated on a chair, if your balance is shaky, and skip the leg wrap if your knee feels twisted.
Intermediate Shoulders Legs Wall or chair optional
Sukhāsana

Easy Seat

Sukhāsana

A simple cross-legged seat used for breathing practice, warm-ups and rest. The hips turn outward and the spine stacks upright over the sitting bones.

Quick cues
  • Sit on the edge of a folded blanket and cross your shins, with each foot resting under the opposite knee.
  • Raise the seat until your knees are level with or lower than your hips, then let your spine grow tall from the sitting bones.
  • If your knees or hips complain, sit on a chair with your feet flat instead, and switch which shin is in front halfway through.
Beginner Hips Spine Blanket or chair optional

Extended Puppy Pose

Uttāna Śiśosana

Halfway between Child's Pose and Downward-Facing Dog: the hips stay over the knees while the arms walk forward and the chest sinks toward the floor. You may notice a stretch through the shoulders and upper back.

Quick cues
  • From hands and knees, keep your hips over your knees and walk your hands forward until your arms are long.
  • Rest your forehead on the mat or a block and let your chest sink, keeping your elbows lifted off the floor.
  • Pad your knees with a blanket, or stand facing a wall with your hands on it and walk your feet back, if your knees or shoulders are tender.
Beginner Shoulders Spine Blanket or wall optional
Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana

Extended Side Angle Pose

Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana

A wide standing pose with the front knee bent and the torso tipping out over the front thigh, making one long line from the back foot to the top hand.

Quick cues
  • From a wide stance, turn your front foot to point forward and your back foot slightly in, then bend your front knee over the ankle.
  • Rest your forearm on your front thigh or your hand on a block, and turn your chest toward the side wall rather than the floor.
  • Keep the front knee in line with the middle of the foot rather than dropping inward, and look down instead of up if your neck feels strained.
Intermediate Legs Hips Block optional

Extended Triangle Pose

Utthita Trikoṇāsana

A wide standing pose with both legs straight, the torso reaching out over the front leg and the arms forming one vertical line. You may notice a stretch along the back of the front leg.

Beginner Hamstrings Legs Block or wall optional Full guide
Malāsana

Garland Pose

Malāsana

A deep squat with the feet a little wider than the hips, the hips low and the elbows resting against the inner knees.

Quick cues
  • Stand with your feet a little wider than your hips and your toes turned out, then bend your knees and lower your hips toward the floor.
  • Bring your palms together at your chest and let your elbows press lightly into your inner knees as your spine lengthens.
  • Sit on a block if your heels lift or your knees ache, or slide a rolled blanket under your heels, and skip the deep squat with a knee injury.
Intermediate Hips Legs Block or blanket optional
Ardha Candrāsana

Half Moon Pose

Ardha Candrāsana

A standing balance on one leg with the torso and the lifted leg parallel to the floor, hips stacked and the top arm reaching up.

Quick cues
  • From Extended Triangle Pose, bend your front knee and place your front hand on a block about 12 inches ahead of your front foot and slightly toward its little-toe side.
  • Straighten the standing leg as you lift the back leg to hip height with the foot flexed, then stack the top hip over the bottom hip.
  • Practice with your back against a wall for support, and keep your gaze down if you feel dizzy or your balance is unsteady.
Advanced Legs Core Block or wall optional
Viparīta Karaṇī

Legs Up the Wall

Viparīta Karaṇī

Lying on your back with your legs resting up a wall. The legs are fully supported, and you may notice a gentle stretch along their backs.

Quick cues
  • Sit side-on to a wall, then swing your legs up it as you lower your back to the floor, bringing your hips close to the wall.
  • Slide your hips a few inches away from the wall if the backs of your legs pull, and rest a folded blanket under your hips if you like.
  • Bend your knees or come out if your feet tingle or go numb, and ask your healthcare provider first if you have glaucoma or are in later pregnancy.
Beginner Hamstrings Legs Wall required
Śalabhāsana

Locust Pose

Śalabhāsana

A face-down backbend in which the chest, arms and legs lift off the floor together. The muscles along the back of the body, from the spine to the hamstrings, hold the lift.

Quick cues
  • Lie on your belly with your arms along your sides, palms down, and your forehead on the mat.
  • On an inhale, lift your chest, arms and legs a few inches, reaching your toes back and keeping the back of your neck long.
  • Fold a blanket under your hips for padding, start by lifting only your chest or only your legs, and skip it during pregnancy or with a recent back injury.
Intermediate Spine Legs Blanket optional
Añjaneyāsana

Low Lunge

Añjaneyāsana

A lunge with the back knee resting on the floor and the front knee bent over the ankle. You may notice a stretch along the front of the back hip and thigh.

Quick cues
  • From hands and knees, step one foot forward between your hands and lower the back knee onto a folded blanket.
  • Stack your front knee over the ankle, then let your hips ease forward as your torso lifts, hands on your front thigh or on blocks.
  • Rest on the thigh just above the back kneecap rather than on the kneecap itself, and practice a standing lunge holding a chair if kneeling hurts.
Beginner Hips Legs Blanket, blocks or chair optional
Tāḍāsana

Mountain Pose

Tāḍāsana

Standing tall with your weight even across both feet. It is the starting shape for most standing poses and a chance to notice how you stand.

Quick cues
  • Stand with your feet hip-width apart and parallel, arms resting by your sides.
  • Spread your weight between your heels and the balls of your feet, and stack your ribs over your hips and your head over your ribs.
  • Keep your knees soft rather than locked, and stand with your back to a wall if you want a reference for upright.
Beginner Legs Core Wall optional

Pigeon Pose

Eka Pāda Rājakapotāsana

A deep hip stretch with the front shin angled across the mat and the back leg extended behind you. The photo shows the upright preparation; the full pose adds a deep backbend.

Quick cues
  • From hands and knees, bring one knee forward behind the same-side wrist and angle that shin across the mat.
  • Slide the back leg straight behind you, and set a block or folded blanket under the front-leg hip so your pelvis stays level.
  • If you feel any twisting at the front knee, lie on your back and cross one ankle over the opposite thigh instead, and skip the pose with a knee or sacroiliac joint injury.
Advanced Hips Block or blanket recommended
Phalakāsana

Plank Pose

Phalakāsana

The top of a push-up: one straight line from head to heels, held up by the hands and toes. The abdominal muscles, shoulders and arms work together.

Quick cues
  • From hands and knees, set your wrists under your shoulders and step your feet back one at a time until your legs are straight.
  • Draw your belly in and keep your hips level with your shoulders, neither sagging toward the floor nor lifting into a peak.
  • Lower your knees to the mat, or press your hands into a wall at chest height, to lighten the load, and skip it with a wrist or shoulder injury.
Intermediate Core Shoulders Wall optional
Supta Baddha Koṇāsana

Reclined Bound Angle Pose

Supta Baddha Koṇāsana

Bound Angle Pose taken onto your back, with the soles of the feet together and the knees open. It is often set up over a bolster so the chest is gently lifted.

Quick cues
  • Sit in front of a bolster or folded blankets laid lengthwise, bring the soles of your feet together, and lie back onto the support.
  • Slide a block or rolled blanket under each outer thigh so your knees are held and nothing in the groin feels strained.
  • Raise the support under your head if your chin tips up, and later in pregnancy keep your torso well propped rather than flat.
Beginner Hips Chest Bolster or blocks optional
Supta Pādāṅguṣṭhāsana

Reclined Hand-to-Big-Toe Pose

Supta Pādāṅguṣṭhāsana

Lying on your back with one leg lifted toward the ceiling and held with a strap. The back of the lifted leg lengthens while the other leg rests on the floor.

Quick cues
  • Lie on your back, bend one knee toward your chest and loop a strap around the ball of that foot, then straighten the leg toward the ceiling.
  • Keep the back of the other leg heavy on the floor and both hips level, holding the strap where your shoulders can stay on the mat.
  • Bend the lifted knee as much as you need, and lower the leg if you feel the stretch behind the knee rather than along the thigh.
Beginner Hamstrings Strap required
Supta Matsyendrāsana

Reclined Twist

Supta Matsyendrāsana

A twist on your back with both knees drawn to one side and the arms open wide. The spine rotates while the floor supports your whole body.

Quick cues
  • Lie on your back, hug your knees in, then lower both knees to one side as your arms open into a T.
  • Keep both shoulders heavy on the floor, and slide a block, bolster or folded blanket under your knees if they hover.
  • Keep the twist small with a back or disc injury, and during pregnancy ask your healthcare provider first and twist only gently with a pillow between your knees.
Beginner Spine Chest Block or bolster optional

Reverse Tabletop

Ardha Pūrvottānāsana

A tabletop facing the ceiling, with hands and feet on the floor, knees bent and hips lifted level with the shoulders and knees. The fronts of the shoulders and chest open while the backs of the arms and legs work.

Quick cues
  • Sit with your knees bent and feet flat, and place your hands on the floor behind you with your fingers pointing toward your feet.
  • Press into your hands and feet to lift your hips until your torso and thighs form a flat line, with your knees over your ankles.
  • Keep your chin slightly tucked if your neck feels strained, and fold a blanket under the heels of your hands, or keep your hips low, if your wrists complain.
Intermediate Chest Shoulders Blanket optional

Seated Forward Bend

Paścimottānāsana

Sitting with your legs extended and folding forward over them. You may notice a stretch along the backs of the legs and the length of the back.

Quick cues
  • Sit on a folded blanket with your legs straight in front of you and feet flexed, and loop a strap around your soles if your hands do not reach.
  • Hinge from the hips with a long spine, leading with your chest rather than pulling your head toward your knees.
  • Bend your knees as much as you need, and avoid rounding hard into the fold if you have a disc injury or a lower back condition.
Beginner Hamstrings Spine Blanket or strap optional

Standing Forward Fold

Uttānāsana

A standing hinge at the hips with the torso hanging toward the legs. The hamstrings and the muscles along the spine lengthen while the head hangs.

Quick cues
  • From Mountain Pose, bend your knees and hinge forward from your hips, resting your hands on your shins, on blocks or on a chair seat.
  • Keep your weight over the balls of your feet and let your head and neck hang completely loose.
  • Rise slowly with bent knees, and keep your head above your heart, hands on a chair, if you have high blood pressure or glaucoma or tend to feel dizzy.
Beginner Hamstrings Spine Blocks or chair optional

Tree Pose

Vṛkṣāsana

A standing balance on one leg with the other foot resting on the inner ankle, calf or thigh. The standing foot, ankle and hip make constant small corrections to keep you upright.

Beginner Legs Hips Wall or chair optional Full guide

Upward-Facing Dog

Ūrdhva Mukha Śvānāsana

A backbend on straight arms with the legs lifted off the floor, so only the hands and the tops of the feet touch the mat. The chest and the fronts of the hips lengthen.

Quick cues
  • Lie on your belly with your hands beside your lower ribs and the tops of your feet on the mat.
  • Press into your hands and straighten your arms as you lift your chest, thighs and knees, rolling your shoulders back and down.
  • Stay in Cobra Pose, or place your hands on blocks, if your lower back pinches or your wrists complain, and skip belly-down poses during pregnancy.
Intermediate Chest Shoulders Blocks optional
Vīrabhadrāsana I

Warrior I

Vīrabhadrāsana I

A standing lunge with the back heel grounded, the hips turned toward the front and the arms reaching overhead. You may notice a stretch along the front of the back hip and the back calf.

Quick cues
  • From standing, step one foot back about 3 to 4 feet and turn the back foot out about 45 degrees with the heel down.
  • Bend your front knee over the ankle, point your hips toward the front as far as is comfortable, and reach your arms up.
  • Widen your stance sideways if your back heel lifts or your lower back pinches, and press your back heel against a wall for steady feedback.
Beginner Legs Hips Wall optional
Vīrabhadrāsana II

Warrior II

Vīrabhadrāsana II

A wide standing pose with the front knee bent, the hips and chest facing the side and the arms stretched out at shoulder height.

Quick cues
  • Step your feet about 4 feet apart, turn your front foot to point forward and your back foot in slightly, and line up your front heel with your back arch.
  • Bend your front knee toward a right angle, keeping it over the ankle and in line with the second toe, and reach your arms long at shoulder height.
  • Shorten your stance if the knee drifts inward, or practice seated sideways on a sturdy chair with your front thigh resting on the seat.
Beginner Legs Hips Chair optional

Practice planning

Build a session from the library

You do not need a script to practice. A simple arc of arriving, warming up, working and resting turns any handful of these cards into a complete session. Here is the order most teachers follow.

  1. Arrive. Start in Easy Seat, on a blanket or a chair, with a minute or two of slow breathing so you can notice how your body feels today.
  2. Warm the spine. Move through Cat-Cow, Extended Puppy Pose and Downward-Facing Dog with bent knees. Small movements come before big ones.
  3. Stand and balance. Choose two to four standing poses, such as Mountain Pose, Warrior II, Extended Triangle Pose and Tree Pose, and practice each one on both sides.
  4. Come to the floor. Add a gentle backbend like Bridge or Cobra, then a seated or reclined hip shape such as Bound Angle Pose, and a reclined twist.
  5. Rest. Finish with at least 3 minutes of Corpse Pose or Legs Up the Wall. Rest is part of the practice, not an optional extra.

Two habits keep a home session balanced: practice every one-sided pose on both sides, and follow a backbend with a neutral or gently rounded shape such as Child's Pose.

Practice with care

The pose library is general movement education, not medical advice. Talk with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition. Choose the supported version whenever you are unsure, stop if you feel pain, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance, especially with intermediate and advanced poses.
