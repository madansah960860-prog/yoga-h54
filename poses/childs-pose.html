Child's Pose

Bālāsana

A kneeling resting shape: the hips sink toward the heels, the torso folds over the thighs and the forehead rests down. It is the pose teachers offer whenever you need a pause, and it has a supported version for knees, ankles and hips that do not enjoy deep bending.

Beginner Resting pose Blanket, bolster or block optional
Level
Beginner
Suggested hold
5 to 10 breaths, or as long as you like
Body area
Back, hips, ankles, shoulders
Props
Optional: blanket, bolster, block or chair

What happens in your body

Child's Pose is a deep fold at the hips, knees and ankles, with the spine gently rounded and the weight of your upper body resting on your thighs and head. Very little has to work, which is why it is used as a rest.

Lengthens

  • Muscles along the spine (erector spinae), as the back rounds
  • Buttock muscles, as the hips bend deeply
  • Fronts of the ankles and tops of the feet
  • Latissimus dorsi, when the arms reach forward

Works, lightly

  • Shoulders and arms, a little, if you reach forward actively
  • The diaphragm and the muscles between the ribs, which you may feel moving the back of your ribcage
  • Almost nothing else, by design

Joints in motion

  • Knees bend fully
  • Hips flex deeply
  • Ankles point, with the tops of the feet on the floor
  • Spine rounds gently forward
  • Neck rests close to neutral when the head is supported

Pose breakdown

Bālāsana
Beginner Hold 1 to 3 minutes

Entry steps

  1. Kneel on your mat with the tops of your feet down and your big toes touching. Pad your knees with a folded blanket if the floor feels hard.
  2. Choose your knee width: together for a rounder back, or about as wide as your mat to make room for your belly and ribs.
  3. Sit your hips back toward your heels. If they do not reach, place a folded blanket or a bolster between your hips and heels.
  4. Walk your hands forward and lower your torso over or between your thighs.
  5. Rest your forehead on the mat, a block or your stacked fists, so your neck is long rather than sharply bent.
  6. Reach your arms forward or rest them alongside your body with palms up, and let your weight settle.
  7. To come out, walk your hands back toward your knees and roll up slowly, lifting your head last.

Alignment cues

  • Let your forehead rest fully on something. If it hovers, raise the floor with a block or a folded blanket.
  • Let your hips sink toward your heels only as far as your knees allow. Never push them down.
  • Soften your shoulders away from your ears, especially with your arms forward.
  • Keep the tops of your feet flat. Roll a small towel or blanket under your ankles if they feel strained.
  • Spread your weight across your shins, thighs and forehead so no single spot carries it all.
  • Unclench your jaw, and close your eyes if that feels comfortable.

Breath pattern

  • Breathe slowly in and out through your nose.
  • On each inhale, notice the back of your ribs and the sides of your waist widen, since your belly is resting on your thighs.
  • On each exhale, let your hips settle a little closer to your heels and your shoulders soften.
  • If your breath feels cramped, widen your knees or slide a bolster under your chest.

Hold duration

5 to 10 breaths about 1 minute

  • As a pause between other poses: a few breaths, or as long as you need.
  • In our guided sequences: usually 1 minute.
  • With a bolster under your torso: up to 3 to 5 minutes, turning your head the other way halfway through.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing the hips onto the heels when the knees protest.
  • Leaving the forehead hovering above the floor, so the neck works the whole time.
  • Gripping the arms forward with the shoulders hunched up by the ears.
  • Staying in the pose through knee or ankle pain because it is "supposed to" be restful.
  • Keeping the knees together when the belly or chest feels squeezed.

Progression

  • Active arms: reach your arms forward, press your palms into the mat and lift your forearms slightly, so the sides of your back lengthen further.
  • Side-reaching Child's Pose: walk both hands toward the right front corner of the mat, hold for 3 breaths, then switch. You may notice a stretch along the left side of your back, then the right.
  • Longer supported holds: with a bolster under your torso, build from 1 minute toward 3 to 5 minutes.

Contraindications and care

  • Use the chair or bolster version, or skip the pose, if you have a knee injury, recent knee surgery or a knee replacement, or if deep kneeling causes pain.
  • After a hip replacement, ask your surgeon or healthcare provider whether deep hip bending is allowed yet before trying the floor version.
  • With an ankle injury or very stiff ankles, roll a blanket under your ankles or use the chair version.
  • During pregnancy, take your knees wide so your belly has room, rest your chest on a bolster, and check with your healthcare provider first.
  • If you have glaucoma or high blood pressure, or feel dizzy with your head low, rest your forehead on stacked blocks or a bolster, or use the chair version.
  • Stop if you feel pain, numbness or tingling in your legs or feet. Work with a qualified teacher in person for hands-on guidance on your setup.

Choose your version

Each of these is Child's Pose. They keep the same rounded, resting shape while changing how much your knees, ankles, hips and neck are asked to give.

Blanket

Blanket-padded Child's Pose

  • Fold a blanket under your knees and shins for cushioning.
  • Roll a second blanket or towel and slide it under the fronts of your ankles if they feel strained.
  • Place a third folded blanket between your hips and heels if they do not meet.
  • The floor changes; the shape of the pose stays exactly the same.
Bolster

Supported Child's Pose

  • Kneel with your knees wide and set a bolster or two firm pillows lengthwise between them.
  • Fold forward so your chest and belly rest on the support, and turn your head to one side.
  • Turn your head the other way halfway through the hold.
  • Your whole torso is held, so longer stays feel easy to settle into.
Block

Forehead on a block

  • Set a block under your forehead at whatever height lets your neck stay long.
  • Stack two blocks, or a block on a folded blanket, if your hips stay high.
  • Use your stacked fists instead if there is no block nearby.
  • Gives your head a place to land when the floor is far away.
Chair

Chair Child's Pose

  • Sit toward the front of a sturdy chair with your feet flat and wider than your hips.
  • Fold forward and rest your chest on a pillow on your lap, letting your arms hang or rest on the floor.
  • Or rest your forearms and forehead on a table in front of you.
  • Keeps the same rounded back with no kneeling at all.

Where this pose shows up

Child's Pose appears in three of our guided sequences, usually as a resting point, and it pairs naturally with the poses below.

10-Minute Morning

Beginner, 10 minutes. A minute of Child's Pose follows Tree Pose, just before a short seated rest.

Evening Wind-Down

Beginner, 20 minutes. Slow, floor-based poses with blocks and a blanket.

Gentle Full Practice

Beginner, 40 minutes. A complete, unhurried class you can follow at home.

Related poses

  • Downward-Facing Dog (Adho Mukha Śvānāsana), which often comes right before Child's Pose in a flowing class
  • Cobra Pose (Bhujaṅgāsana), a gentle backbend that Child's Pose naturally follows as a rounded counter-shape
  • Bridge Pose (Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana), another floor pose, this time lifting the hips as the back of the body works
  • Find Extended Puppy Pose, Reclined Bound Angle Pose and other resting shapes in the Pose Library
