Tree Pose

Vṛkṣāsana

A one-legged standing balance with the sole of the lifted foot resting against the standing leg. The foot, ankle and hip you stand on make hundreds of tiny corrections every minute, and the pose works just as well with a hand on the wall.

Beginner Standing balance Wall or chair optional
Level
Beginner
Suggested hold
5 to 8 breaths each side, about 30 to 45 seconds
Body area
Feet, ankles, legs, hips
Props
Optional: wall or sturdy chair

What happens in your body

Tree Pose is a single-leg stance. The standing leg holds you up and keeps your pelvis level, while the lifted leg bends and turns outward at the hip. Here is what works, what lengthens and what keeps you upright.

Works

  • Gluteus medius and minimus on the standing side, which keep the pelvis level
  • Muscles of the standing foot, ankle and lower leg, making constant small corrections
  • Quadriceps, keeping the standing knee straight without locking it
  • Deep abdominal and back muscles that keep the torso stacked

Lengthens

  • Inner thigh muscles (adductors) of the lifted leg
  • Front of the lifted hip, gently, as the knee opens out
  • Sides of the torso and the latissimus dorsi, when the arms reach overhead

Joints in motion

  • Lifted hip bends, opens to the side and rotates outward
  • Lifted knee bends deeply
  • Standing ankle shifts in tiny movements
  • Shoulders flex if the arms go overhead

How you balance

  • Pressure sensors in the sole of your standing foot
  • Your eyes, which is why a still gaze point matters
  • The balance organs of your inner ear
  • Wobbling is these systems doing their job, not a mistake

Pose breakdown

Vṛkṣāsana
Beginner Hold 30 to 45 seconds a side

Entry steps

  1. Stand in Mountain Pose with your feet hip-width apart and your weight even across both feet. For support, stand side-on to a wall, about an arm's length away.
  2. Shift your weight into your left foot. Spread the toes and feel the heel, the base of the big toe and the base of the little toe all pressing down.
  3. Keep your left knee soft rather than locked, and rest your gaze on one point that does not move, about 4 to 6 feet ahead on the floor or at eye level on a wall.
  4. Turn your right knee out to the side. Place the right sole on your left ankle with the toes on the floor, on your inner calf, or on your inner thigh above the knee. Use your hand to place the foot if that is easier.
  5. Press the sole and the standing leg into each other with equal effort, then bring your palms together at your chest or rest your hands on your hips.
  6. To come out, lower the foot with control, stand evenly for one breath, and repeat with the left foot lifted.

Alignment cues

  • Keep the foot off the side of the knee joint. Ankle, calf and thigh are all complete versions of the pose.
  • Draw the standing hip in toward your midline so it does not jut out to the side.
  • Point both hip bones forward like headlights. The lifted knee can angle slightly forward to make this possible.
  • Let your tailbone drop toward the floor so your lower back stays long rather than arched.
  • Stack your ribs over your pelvis and let your shoulders soften away from your ears.
  • If the foot slides on smooth leggings, move it lower to the calf or ankle rather than gripping harder.

Breath pattern

  • Breathe slowly in and out through your nose.
  • On each inhale, feel your ribs widen and the crown of your head lift.
  • On each exhale, let the standing foot spread and your shoulders soften.
  • If you catch yourself holding your breath to stay up, touch the wall and let the breath move again. Many students find the balance settles once the breath does.

Hold duration

5 to 8 breaths about 30 to 45 seconds a side

  • New to the pose: 3 to 5 breaths on each side, with a hand on the wall.
  • With regular practice: build toward 1 minute on each side.
  • Always practice both sides, and expect one to feel steadier than the other. That is normal.

Common mistakes

  • Pressing the foot into the side of the knee, which pushes the joint sideways.
  • Letting the standing hip sink out to the side so your weight hangs in the hip joint.
  • Locking the standing knee straight back.
  • Clenching the toes to grip the floor instead of spreading them.
  • Forcing the lifted knee so far back that the pelvis twists open.
  • Watching something that moves, such as a screen, a pet or another person.

Progression

  • Higher foot placement: move from ankle to calf to inner thigh, and only when the lower placement feels steady for the whole hold.
  • Branches overhead: on an inhale, reach your arms up shoulder-width apart or into a wide V, keeping your shoulders down and your ribs stacked.
  • Eyes closed at the wall: standing right beside a wall, close your eyes for 2 breaths. Your foot and inner ear now do all the balancing, so keep a hand ready.

Contraindications and care

  • Use the wall or a chair if you are prone to dizziness or vertigo, have an inner ear condition, have limited feeling in your feet (for example from neuropathy), or take medication that affects your balance.
  • After a recent ankle, knee or hip injury, or a joint replacement, check with your healthcare provider first, then start with the kickstand or seated chair version.
  • Keep your hands at your chest or hips rather than overhead if you have high blood pressure or a shoulder injury.
  • During pregnancy, your center of gravity shifts week by week. Practice beside a wall or chair and ask your healthcare provider first.
  • Stop if you feel pain in the knee, hip or ankle, or any numbness or tingling. Work with a qualified teacher in person for hands-on guidance on your alignment.

Choose your version

Each of these is Tree Pose. They keep the same rooted standing leg and outward-turned hip while changing how much your balance has to manage on its own. Pick the one that suits your body today.

Wall

Tree at the wall

  • Stand side-on to a wall, about an arm's length away, and rest your fingertips or palm on it at shoulder height.
  • Stand on the leg nearer the wall and lift the other foot, so the lifted knee opens away from the wall.
  • Or stand with your back against the wall and let the lifted knee rest on it.
  • Keep the hand light: it is a reference point, not something to lean on.
Chair

Chair-held Tree

  • Stand beside the back of a sturdy chair without wheels and hold the top of it with one hand.
  • Lift the foot on the side away from the chair and place it at ankle, calf or thigh height.
  • Rest your free hand on your hip or at your chest.
  • Useful when there is no clear wall space where you practice.
Seat

Seated Tree

  • Sit toward the front of a sturdy chair with both feet flat and hip-width apart.
  • Turn your right knee out and draw the right foot in so its sole rests against your left ankle or shin.
  • Sit tall over your sitting bones with palms together at your chest, or reach your arms up.
  • Keeps the same hip shape on days when standing on one leg is not available.
Toes

Kickstand Tree

  • From Mountain Pose, turn one knee out to the side.
  • Rest that heel against your inner standing ankle and keep the toes on the floor, like a bicycle kickstand.
  • Press through the toes only as much as you need, and let the standing leg take more weight over time.
  • Many experienced students begin every Tree Pose this way to find their balance first.

Where this pose shows up

Tree Pose appears in two of our guided sequences, and it pairs naturally with the poses below.

10-Minute Morning

Beginner, 10 minutes. Tree Pose on each side comes just before Child's Pose and a short seated rest.

Gentle Full Practice

Beginner, 40 minutes. A complete, unhurried class you can follow at home.

Related poses

  • Extended Triangle Pose (Utthita Trikoṇāsana), another standing pose built on steady, rooted feet
  • Bridge Pose (Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana), which works the backs of the hips and thighs from the floor
  • Downward-Facing Dog (Adho Mukha Śvānāsana), a four-limbed shape that often comes before standing work
